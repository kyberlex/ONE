The vibration coming through the floorboards of the dispatch office was not a hum; it was a rhythmic, bone-deep shudder that set the cold coffee in Giulia Bellini’s tin mug shivering in concentric rings. Below the mezzanine grates, down in the dripping dark of the pit, the turbine casing had begun to weep under four hundred meters of mountain head. She could hear Davide coughing through the floor vents—a wet, rattling bark—and the sharp, furious bark of Matteo’s voice driving wedges against the seating collar.

Giulia’s left hand would not stay flat on the desk. 

She pressed her palm into the gray laminate until her wrist went white, but the tremor crept up the tendons of her forearm anyway, an old, persistent telegraph from the base of her neck. When she was nine, before the debt courts took her father’s dry-cleaning shop on Corso Francia, her grandmother had tried to cure those involuntary twitches with scalded milk and warm flannel wrapped around her wrist. In this mountain loft, there was only the smell of ozone, scorched transformer varnish, and the bitter alpine draft cutting under the unsealed door frame.

Across the trestle table, Alexandre Favre leaned his forehead against the cold zinc casing of the air-gapped terminal, his fingers jammed into his eyes. Fluorescent light flickered overhead, chewing at the edges of his migraine. 

"The Swiss relay is dropping packets again," he muttered, his voice scraped raw from two nights without sleep. "The snow over the pass is thick as curd. The laser can barely burn through the squall."

"Keep it locked on Mount Rocciamelone," Giulia said. Her voice sounded thin, stripped of moisture. She reached for the paper feed rattling out of the matrix printer. The ribbon was dying, stamping pale purple text across cheap accordion-folded pulp, but the German judicial seal on the header was unmistakably sharp. "Frankfurt didn't wait for the morning docket."

Alexandre pulled his head up, squinting through the glare. "The tribunal in Turin granted the hearing?"

"They bypassed Turin entirely," she said, tracking the faint purple lines with a thumb that threatened to tear the paper. "Vanguard filed under the European cross-border critical infrastructure protocols. An emergency enforcement petition lodged in Hesse at two in the morning. They’re treating the Cenisio intake not as a civic commons, but as a compromised collateral asset in sovereign default."

"They can’t seize municipal public works on an unadjudicated commercial claim," Alexandre spat. "Article Eleven of the Hague trust convention—"

"The trust convention means nothing if the underlying asset is classified as an instrument of state sabotage," Giulia cut him off. Her stomach dropped, a cold, hollow plummet that carried the exact chemical taste of her recurring nightmare: the gray iron gates of Le Vallette penitentiary on the western ring road of Turin, the metallic snap of institutional deadbolts echoing down concrete tiers where the sun never reached. She had watched three union organizers vanish into those processing cells during the rail strikes of 2021. None of them had seen a magistrate for eleven months. 

She turned the sheet. Her tremor made the paper rattle against the metal desk trim like dried leaves. "They didn't just ask for an asset freeze, Alexandre. They attached an emergency European Arrest Warrant. Fast-track criminal enforcement. Inter-state police assistance."

"Names?"

"Mine," she said. The word was flat, gray. "Matteo’s. Lukas’s. And they’ve named 'unidentified domestic actors' to cover every apprentice and valve-tender in the valley."

Alexandre cursed softly in Arpitan, his hand instinctively reaching for the power disconnect toggle on the switchboard behind him. "If the Carabinieri at the crossroads get that dispatch over their onboard terminals—"

"Marshal Moretti won't act on a Frankfurt commercial warrant," Giulia said, though the certainty in her chest felt like wet sand collapsing underfoot. "Moretti knows the valley freeze will kill thirty pensioners in the lower blocks if the turbine trips. But Moretti isn't the only uniform on the state road tonight."

She dragged the cursor across the secondary monitor, calling up the forensic audit tranches they had pulled through the Swiss laser link before the storm thickened. 

For three days, the narrative fed to the regional press had been simple: the valley community had pirated a decommissioned hydroelectric station belonging to the regional utility’s receivership, defaulting on thirty million euros in historic operating debt. But when Giulia drilled down past the Luxembourg holding wrappers into the core balance sheet, the math had refused to cohere. 

Hydroelectric plants do not accumulate thirty million euros in operational losses while their penstocks are dry and their gates are locked. The numbers were too heavy, their maturities staggered in bizarre twenty-eight-day cycles that had nothing to do with seasonal snowmelt or water rights.

"Look at this," Giulia commanded, her voice dropping into the precise, razor-edged register she had used during her brief, miserable year clerking for the administrative court in Milan. 

Alexandre dragged his chair over, the steel casters screeching across the concrete. "What am I looking at? It’s just debt servicing tranches. Bundled public credit."

"No," she said, her fingernail scoring the glass screen under a cluster of identical account codes. "It’s not industrial debt. Look at the originating debtor identifiers. Prefix *TO-SAN-LUIGI*. That’s not the regional grid operator."

Alexandre blinked, the bluish screen light carving deep hollows beneath his cheekbones. "San Luigi Gonzaga? The regional hospital complex in Orbassano?"

"The pediatric intensive care unit and the oncology pharmacy," Giulia said, the words falling like lead weights into the freezing room. "Three years ago, during the procurement squeeze, the regional health trust couldn't settle its liquid nitrogen and specialized antibiotic invoices. A commercial supplier in Munich factored the unpaid hospital bills out to an off-balance-sheet vehicle. Vanguard bought the paper for seven cents on the euro."

Alexandre stared at the scrolling column of ledger entries. "Why would a Frankfurt debt fund bundle unpaid pediatric oxygen bills into an alpine hydroelectric concession?"

"Because public hospital debt carries a statutory sovereign guarantee under Italian civil law," Giulia whispered. Her left hand had finally stopped shaking, locked instead into a rigid, painful claw. "If the health authority defaults, the creditor can attach any unencumbered regional asset of equivalent liquidation value. Vanguard didn't just buy the hospital debt to collect the interest. They parked it inside the shell company that held the defunct Cenisio water lease. Then, six weeks ago, they intentionally triggered a technical covenant breach."

"How?"

"By rejecting the regional health trust’s scheduled installment," she said, bringing up the clearinghouse timestamps. "The Piedmont treasury tried to wire four hundred thousand euros on the fifteenth of last month. Vanguard’s clearing agent in Dublin bounced the payment back, citing an 'administrative identity discrepancy' in the hospital’s bank routing code. Forty-eight hours later, they declared the entire thirty-million-euro facility in non-curable cross-default."

Alexandre let out a sharp, incredulous breath that plumed in the cold air. "They manufactured the insolvency. They choked off the hospital's clearance so they could drop the hammer on the hydro intake before the winter freeze."

"They didn't just manufacture it," Giulia said, her throat tightening until she could barely draw the mountain air into her lungs. "They used the fabricated default to secure a high-priority lien on the high-head penstock. When we opened the bypass flume three nights ago to bring heat back to the commune’s clinic, we weren't just trespassing on a dormant corporate lease. Under the paperwork Vanguard filed in Frankfurt, we were physically embezzling the collateral backing the medical supply lines of the entire metropolitan area of Turin."

It was a trap of monstrous, bureaucratic perfection. If the commune held the plant, Vanguard would parade them before every television camera from Milan to Rome as criminal saboteurs who had stolen the collateral guaranteeing pediatric medicine. If the commune yielded the plant, Vanguard would shut the gates, drain the intake into the lower diversion canals, and let the valley freeze until the regional council surrendered the entire watershed to private infrastructure concessions.

Downstairs, the rhythmic clanking in the turbine hall suddenly ceased. 

The silence that followed was terrifying. It wasn't the peaceful quiet of an alpine night; it was the dead, suffocating vacuum of heavy machinery brought to an unnatural, screeching halt. 

Giulia sprang to the office railing. "Matteo!"

The only answer was the violent hiss of high-pressure steam and the flat, sickening slap of a pressurized water jet striking the pit wall. 

Then, from the desktop transceiver behind her, the shortwave radio crackled, spitting bursts of white noise through its small oval speaker. 

"Giulia," Marshal Moretti’s voice broke through, distorted by mountain static and the Doppler wail of heavy diesel engines climbing the switchbacks. "Giulia, do you copy me? Get off the plant floor."

She lunged back across the room, snatching the handset. "Marshal? What’s happening at the bridge?"

"It’s not my men," Moretti’s breath came in ragged, furious gasps. "The prefect just signed an emergency jurisdictional override out of Turin. We’ve been relieved of perimeter duty. AlpSec brought three armored troop carriers through the lower toll gate five minutes ago. They aren't waiting for the morning hearing. They have federal marshals with them, and they've got the Frankfurt arrest warrant in hand."

The dispatch monitor on Alexandre’s desk flickered once, then flashed an unyielding, high-contrast banner of amber text across the forensic screen:

**SCHENGEN INFORMATION SYSTEM — LEVEL 1 PRIORITY INTERDICTION**
**SUBJECT: BELLINI, GIULIA**
**DIRECTIVE: IMMEDIATE EXTRA-TERRITORIAL DETENTION**

Through the high clerestory windows of the generator hall, two sweeping white searchlight beams cut through the blizzard, reflecting off the glacial granite cliffs above the penstock gantry, turning the falling snow into a blizzard of falling ash. The heavy hum of twelve-cylinder diesel engines was already vibrating through the stone foundations, drowning out the dying hiss of the Pelton wheel.
