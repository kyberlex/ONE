The wind howling down the Cenisio gorge smelled of sheared granite and wet iron. 

Forty meters up the exterior service gantry, the steel grating under Matteo’s boots vibrated with the shudder of six hundred vertical meters of trapped mountain water. His right knee burned—a sharp, grinding ache that flared with every rung he ascended, the old souvenir of a shunting yard derailment thirty years back. He kept his head ducked inside his heavy canvas collar, teeth clamped shut against the bitter gale. Behind his ear, tucked into the brim of his wool cap out of long rail-yard habit, rested a flat carpenter’s pencil, its wood swollen by the damp. 

"Look up!" Lukas shouted through the roar of the blizzard. 

Lukas clung to the safety line above him, his yellow climber’s harness cinched tight over a grease-stained parka. The two deadened fingers on his left hand—an old frostbite gift from a winter spent stringing municipal water pipe across the Oberland—were clawed tight into the rungs. With his right arm, he pointed toward the sheer dark scarp of the upper gorge.

Through the driven snow, three tiny, hovering pinpricks of green light drifted downward like hornets circling an open wound. 

Drones. Heavy industrial quads with carbon-shrouded props, their high-pitched whine eaten by the wind until they were barely fifty paces out. Below their bellies, thermal imaging pods glowed with dull amber lenses, scanning the black cylinder of the penstock. 

"They’re not aiming for the station doors," Lukas yelled, his breath tearing away in ragged plumes. "They're going for the suspension collars!"

Matteo squinted into the dark. A hundred paces above their heads, where the massive riveted pipe crossed a cleft in the chasm, four figures clung to the ice-glazed rock face. They moved with the terrifying, rhythmic economy of professional mountain combatants—dark alpine storm-suits, crampons biting into the frozen gneiss, tethered to high-tensile static lines dropped from the rim. Two of them carried pressurized packs with rigid hoses trailing to short cutting wands. 

A sharp hiss cracked through the gorge. A blinding blue-white arc erupted against the upper cable mount, throwing the jagged ribs of the mountain into violent relief. AlpSec wasn't bothering with the fortified steel doors of the generator hall. If they severed the two primary tension collars that held the high-head line to the cliff face, the unbraced weight of the water column would shear the lower expansion joints. The penstock would peel away like a split artery, wiping the powerhouse—and everyone inside it—into the riverbed below.

"The manual bypass!" Matteo barked, lunging onto the mid-tier gantry. 

"What?"

"The bypass!" Matteo hammered his gloved fist against an iron wheel the size of a wagon tire, bolted to a massive diversion valve branching off the main column. "We don't have to touch them! We open the head!"

Lukas scrambled onto the grated platform, his eyes tracking the pipe. "If you crack the relief bypass at this head pressure, the divergent cone dumps straight into the flume runout!"

"Look where the runout points, boy!"

Lukas looked. The emergency pressure nozzle—designed eighty years ago to vent shockwaves during sudden turbine trips—faced out across the chasm, aimed directly through the narrow neck of the gorge where the commando team hung suspended. 

A thermal drone dropped out of the spindrift ten yards away, its search-beam locking onto the platform. A high-intensity white beam pinned them against the rusted steel plate, blinding Matteo in an instant. A warning burst of rubber-coated riot rounds peppered the safety cage, clattering against the metal like hail. 

Matteo didn't flinch; he dropped his shoulder against the iron spoke of the bypass wheel. "Cheater bar! Behind the junction box! Move!"

Lukas dove across the wet grating, his boots skidding on black ice. He grabbed a four-foot length of galvanized scaffolding pipe, dragging it back through the pelting snow. The search-beam trailed him, blinding and unrelenting, while above them, the blue cutting torch spat a fountain of orange sparks against the suspension cable. The steel cable groaned—a sickening, low-frequency twang that resonated through the cliff.

"Into the spokes!" Matteo wheezed, his chest rattling with a dry, ragged cough. "Lukas! Now!"

Lukas jammed the hollow steel pipe between two spokes of the wheel. The valve was frozen fast, locked by days of sub-zero crust and stagnant seepage.

"Together!" Lukas yelled.

Matteo gripped the galvanized pipe, throwing the entire dead weight of his torso into the leverage. Lukas hauled upward with both arms, his good hand locked white, the frostbitten fingers slipping twice against the slick zinc. 

"Heave!"

The iron screeched—a violent, tearing groan of stripped rime. The wheel lurched forward two inches. 

"Again!" 

A second drone dropped down, hovering just above the gantry railing. Its prop wash kicked an icy vortex of grit and snow straight into their faces. Matteo's knee buckled under the strain, his boot heel catching in the open mesh of the grating. He went down on one knee, agony shooting up his thigh, but he never let go of the bar. 

"Don't look at the light!" Matteo screamed, his voice raw. "Turn it! Turn it until the stem clears the seat!"

Lukas threw his body across the pipe, his ribs slamming into the metal. 

With a deafening *thud* that shook the entire catwalk, the bypass gate cracked open. 

Inside the six-foot steel pipe, six hundred meters of vertical mountain water found an exit. The sound wasn't a roar; it was a physical blow, a concussive shockwave of compressed atmosphere that blew the nearest drone ten feet sideways into the granite rock face, its carbon blades shattering against stone.

From the flared divergent nozzle below the gantry, a solid pillar of glacial water—five feet thick, compressed under sixty atmospheres of raw gravity—erupted into the empty chasm. 

It hit the frozen air at minus fourteen degrees.

The high-pressure jet didn't simply spray; it atomized. The freezing air shattered the water column into a violent, expanding hurricane of blinding mist, a fifty-meter curtain of supercooled fog that surged upward through the chasm like a volcanic plume. 

Instantly, the world vanished into white.

The drone beams died—not because their bulbs broke, but because the atomized cloud was so dense the light turned into an impenetrable wall of glare. Above the gantry, the wind whipped the freezing plume across the face of the cliff. 

At that temperature, atomized glacial water didn't stay liquid. It flash-froze the second it struck a cold surface.

A cry of shock echoed down from the rock face, sharp and human, ripped away by the howling mist. 

Lukas shielded his face, peering upward through the stinging ice needles. 

The cutting torch was out. The blue arc died with a sharp pop as gallons of atomized water choked the gas nozzles. On the rock face, the four AlpSec commandos were suddenly fighting not an enemy, but the sudden, terrifying weight of the mountain itself. 

The supercooled mist struck their high-tensile ropes and froze instantly, turning the flexible synthetic braids into rigid, brittle cables of solid rime, three times their normal thickness. Their Gore-Tex outer shells iced over in seconds, crusting their arms and legs in stiff, glassy armor that locked their elbow joints. Their helmets and polycarbonate visors were instantly coated in an opaque skin of white frost, blinding them completely.

"My line!" a voice shouted from the cliff, hoarse with sudden, primal panic. "Rope's jammed! Ascender won't slide!"

"Purge the rig! Hit the emergency release!" another screamed, his command broken by the violent thrashing of wind and water. 

One of the commandos, his pack weighted down by forty pounds of rapidly accumulating rime, began to slide uncontrollably as his iced ascender teeth failed to bite into the frozen sheath. He caught himself twenty feet lower on a relief ledge, his boots skidding wildly on rock that had just been glazed in a quarter-inch of smooth, fatal ice. 

They weren't retreating out of discipline; they were fleeing for their lives against the freezing physics of the gorge. Through the blinding white fog, Lukas saw two of them punch the manual brake rings on their descenders, dropping like lead weights through the spray toward the lower scree slope, desperate to get below the freezing plume before their gear locked completely into stone.

The remaining thermal drone, its motors whining in a high-pitched, dying shriek, listed heavily to the port side. The supercooled mist had coated its rotors in uneven wedges of ice; the gyros lost stability, wobbling violently before the drone inverted and plunged like a dead rock into the roaring flume below.

Matteo didn't stop. Leaning over the frozen wheel, he eased the pipe back half a turn, stabilizing the valve stem so the pressure wouldn't water-hammer the Pelton manifold back inside the plant. 

The spray held. A towering, freezing geyser that walled off the entire gorge, cutting the lower valley road off from the upper scarp with a wall of impassable, bone-cracking ice. 

Lukas sank back against the wet steel plate of the gantry, his breath coming in violent, shallow gasps. His hands were shaking so hard he could barely open his fingers. The canvas of his parka was stiff, frozen into a hard shell that crunched whenever he moved his shoulders. 

Matteo slid down beside him, his bad knee stretched out straight along the grating, his knuckles raw and bleeding where the skin had stuck to the freezing iron. He pulled the swollen drafting pencil from his cap, checked that the lead wasn't snapped, and slipped it slowly back into the brim. 

"They're off the face," Lukas whispered, his teeth chattering uncontrollably. "They dropped to the valley track. They won't climb that again tonight. Not in this."

Matteo wiped a crust of ice from his eyelashes and looked down. 

Through the roaring white veil of the water curtain, down on the switchback two hundred meters below, the headlights of the three AlpSec troop carriers had stopped. Their searchlights were sweeping blindly into the base of the ice plume, illuminating a swirling, towering wall of frozen fog that completely blocked the bridge approach. 

Then, high above them on the dark peak of Mount Rocciamelone, a single red flare arced into the night sky, cutting through the blizzard toward the Swiss frontier. 

From the shortwave transceiver hooked to Matteo’s belt, Alexandre’s voice broke through the static, taut with sudden, terrifying alarm.

"Matteo! Lukas! Get down here right now!"

Matteo keyed the mic with a stiff thumb. "We held the penstock, Alex. The gorge is frozen solid."

"It’s not the gorge!" Alexandre shouted, the audio tearing as sirens began to wail inside the plant control room behind him. "The optical laser link to Valais just went dark. Someone just cut the high-altitude relay on the Swiss crest."
