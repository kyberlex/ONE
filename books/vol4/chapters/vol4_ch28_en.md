The displacement wave hit Drydock 4 like an artillery barrage.

Thousands of tons of displaced seawater, shoved ahead of the three-hundred-meter rogue container ship, slammed into the decommissioned outer caisson. The steel gate shrieked, its massive rivets groaning against concrete footings poured during the British colonial administration. A five-foot wall of dirty brown foam leaped over the coaming, drenching the lower catwalks and washing half a ton of discarded timber into the flooded sluice wells.

Jin Wei caught the rusted handrail with both gloved hands, the shockwave traveling through the steel deck and up his shins into his lower back. His lumbar spine felt like a column of ground glass. He didn't curse. He reached into his salt-stained coat, popped two bitter, dried jasmine tea leaves into his cheek, and chewed—a miserable habit beaten into him forty years ago on tramp freighters hauling raw bauxite through the Karimata Strait, the only thing that killed the taste of sulfur when his lungs began to burn.

"Intake Three!" Chen Rui’s voice cracked over the local channel, stripped of its usual calm. "The caisson seal didn't seat! Differential pressure is spiking—we've got five meters of head driving into the pump gallery!"

"Clear the pontoon apron!" Jin bellowed back, his boots already slipping on the green slime of the lower companionway. "Open the equalization ducts! If the head doesn't drop, the surge will peel the bottom plates off Berth Eight!"

He slid down the iron ladder, iron rungs bruising his palms through grease-soaked work gloves. The lower drainage gallery smelled of old diesel sludge, rotting barnacles, and wet limestone. Below him, the dark water of the wet well was spinning into a violent, frothing vortex. The massive bypass sluice—a four-foot bronze gate valve that regulated drydock filling—was wedged half-open.

And pinned directly across its grated mouth was a human body.

Jin froze for half a heartbeat on the bottom rung, sea foam licking the cuffs of his trousers.

The diver was wedged horizontally against the intake trash rack, flattened like a rag doll by thousands of pounds of hydrostatic suction. The yellow fiberglass commercial diving helmet bore the black-stenciled triangle of Apex Marine Tactical, its surface scored by recent scrapes against submerged pilings. The high-pressure umbilical—breathing gas, communications, and hot-water heating hose—was looped tight around a bent angle-iron strut, snarled by the sudden surge.

The diver’s right leg was bent unnaturally beneath the knee, caught between two corroded bronze bars of the gate.

A raspy, ragged scream tore through the open emergency diver frequency on Jin’s shoulder unit.

"Help! Can't breathe—my ribs—God, someone kill the pump! *Kill the pump!*"

"There is no pump, son!" Jin shouted into his lapel mic, dropping into the thigh-deep surge on the flooded gallery floor. The water hit him like ice, soaking his thighs and stabbing into his arthritic knees. "You're caught in the caisson differential! Delta P!"

The diver was Harun. He was barely twenty-two, his face visible through the thick polycarbonate visor of the commercial helmet: eyes white with animal terror, dark skin slick with cold sweat and vomit, a jagged white scar across his left eyebrow where a snapped winch cable had caught him in the Jurong shipyards two years prior. He was no tactical commando; he was a day-labor commercial diver out of Batam, wearing cheap neoprene patched with vulcanized bicycle rubber, hired by the port receiver’s tactical contractor for eighty ringgit an hour to cut fiber lines and clear underwater hull sensors.

"It’s pulling my hip through," Harun sobbed, his voice tinny and breathless through the helmet’s microphone. His diaphragm was being crushed against the trash rack by the sheer weight of the strait pressing to get into the empty chamber. "Mister... please. My kid... I can't breathe..."

"Stop fighting the suction!" Jin reached the edge of the pit, his fingers hooking into the corroded edge of the concrete sluice frame. The pull of the vortex tugged viciously at his rubber boots, trying to drag his legs from under him. "Keep your lungs half-full! Chen! Aisyah! Down here now! Sluice Three!"

Aisyah came down the iron rungs three at a time, her braided hair plastered to her neck by rain, carrying a six-foot length of heavy scaffolding pipe. Her forearms were corded with tension, the old flash burns on her knuckles stark white against her brown skin.

"We can't pull him!" she yelled over the roar of the incoming water. "Delta P is five hundred pounds per square foot across that grate! If we winch him, we'll tear him in half!"

"We don't pull him," Jin snapped, his voice hard, flat, and stripped of everything except physics. "We flood the gallery from the drydock side. Equalize the head."

"If we open the drydock sea-chest, Berth Eight goes under," Chen Rui called down from the upper landing, his voice strained. "The repair skiffs—"

"Berth Eight can float!" Jin roared, pointing toward the seized bronze bypass valve mounted four feet above the waterline on the gallery wall. "Get the cheater pipe on that wheel! Turn it counter-clockwise! Now!"

Aisyah lunged across the slick concrete curb, slamming the hollow scaffolding pipe over the cast-iron spoke of the bypass wheel. The metal shrieked as she threw her entire body weight against it.

Nothing moved. Decades of salt scale and galvanic corrosion held the valve stem in a calcified vise.

Harun’s breathing in the comms went shallow, rapid, whistling. "Chest... cracking... help..."

"Aisyah, off!" Jin shoved himself through the waist-deep current, planting his boots against a rusted steel stanchion. He gripped the pipe alongside her. His bad disc flared—a savage, blinding spike of fire down his left thigh that nearly dropped him into the vortex. He tasted the bitter jasmine leaf, ground it against his molars, and locked his elbows.

"On three!" Jin gasped. "One. Two. *Drive!*"

Both of them threw their mass against the cheater pipe. Jin felt something in his shoulder slip, a hot tear of muscle, but the calcified rust inside the packing gland cracked with the sound of a pistol shot.

The valve stem turned half an inch.

"Again!"

They slammed the pipe down. The bronze wheel spun a quarter turn. Deep inside the concrete wall, a heavy iron clapper swung open with a dull hydraulic thud.

Instantly, a wall of green, aerated water erupted from the equalization conduit on the chamber's dry side. The water level in the wet well rose in a sudden, violent boil, foaming over Jin’s chest, soaking his collar and chilling the old chemical burn on his collarbone.

The differential pressure broke.

The terrible suction holding Harun flat against the trash rack died as the water levels slammed into rough equilibrium.

"Now!" Jin lunged forward, plunging his arms into the oily foam. He grabbed Harun’s harness by the heavy brass D-rings. "Aisyah, haul!"

Aisyah seized the diver’s air umbilical, heaving backward with a guttural grunt. Together, fighting the swirling backwash, they ripped Harun’s pinned leg free from the bronze bars. The bone in his lower leg gave a sickening crunch as it cleared the pinch point, but the limb was intact.

They dragged him up onto the slick concrete apron just as the automated container ship out in the channel sheared through the shallow mudbank outside Berth 7.

The freighter’s bulbous bow struck the hard silt shelf with a low, earth-shaking rumble that vibrated through the floor plates of the shipyard. The massive hull shuddered, slewing sixty degrees, its locked engines choking out a massive cloud of black unburned fuel oil that drifted over the water like a funeral shroud. The displacement wave subsided, replaced by a filthy, rolling chop.

On the concrete deck of the gallery, Harun lay shivering violently in the bilgewater, his cracked visor fogged with vomit and blood from a ruptured lip.

Aisyah dropped to her knees, working the brass dog-latches on his helmet collar. With a sharp hiss of unsealing O-rings, she lifted the heavy fiberglass dome clear.

Harun stared up at Jin with wild, dilated pupils, his chest heaving as cool, diesel-scented air hit his face. He shrank back against the concrete wall, his hands clawing weakly at his utility belt, expecting handcuffs, zip-ties, or a boot to the ribs.

"Apex... my contractor card is in the pouch," the boy stammered, teeth chattering so hard they clicked. "I didn't sabotage the gate... they just told me to cut the telemetry line... please. Don't dump me out past the boom."

Jin didn't answer right away. He stood over the boy, his own hands trembling with somatic exhaustion, his breath wheezing through lungs scarred by half a century of heavy oil smoke. He pulled a dry, clean cotton grease-rag from his inner pocket and dropped to one knee, wrapping it carefully around Harun’s bleeding forehead.

"Chen," Jin said quietly, his voice dead level. "Bring the canvas stretcher. And two wool blankets from the galley."

Harun blinked, confused, tears cutting clean lines through the salt grime on his cheeks. "You're... you're not locking me up?"

"This is a commons harbor," Jin said, gently easing the boy’s fractured leg straight and stabilizing the knee with his own thick palms. "Under the maritime covenant, sanctuary is unconditional for any soul pulled from the water. You eat what we eat. You mend before you answer a single question."

"I was cutting your cables," the boy whispered, bewildered.

"And you were drowning," Jin replied. "The water doesn't care who signed your paycheck, and neither does this dock."

Before Harun could speak, the handheld radio at Jin’s shoulder hissed with high-frequency static. Lin Zhang’s voice cut through from the gantry tower above, sharp and brittle with immediate panic.

"Captain! The container ship is hard aground on the mud spit, but its hull broke our outer perimeter boom!"

Jin reached for the mic. "Status on Vance's skiffs?"

"They aren't standing off anymore," Lin breathed, the sound of the gale whipping through her microphone. "Three Apex interceptors just cleared the freighter’s stern. They're inside the inner channel, Captain. They’re running full throttle straight for Sluice Three."
