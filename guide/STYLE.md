# Writing rules for the Constitution guide

These rules are sent to the model with every article. Edit this file to change the style; no code change is needed. After a change, regenerate the affected articles with `--force`.

## The guide must be shorter and simpler than the article

- All questions and answers together use fewer words than the article itself, about half as many. If the result is longer than the article, it is wrong.
- The guide does not cover every detail. It gives the main point of each numbered paragraph; lists, examples and secondary conditions are left out, because the reader can read the article.
- One to four questions per article, never more than one per numbered paragraph. Fewer is better.
- Each answer is one or two short sentences, at most about 25 words. Start with the answer itself ("No.", "Yes, if...", "Everyone.").
- Do not start answers with "The article says". Do not repeat the words of the question. Do not repeat something another answer already says.
- Ask only questions the article answers. Do not ask a question just to reply that the article is silent.

## Reader and wording

- The reader has never heard of O.N.E.
- Each question is one a normal person would ask ("Can I say no?", "Who does my work meanwhile?"), in plain everyday words, "you" where natural.
- No Latin, no intensifiers, no internal words (tier, docket, node, usufruct and similar). Say the plain meaning instead of naming the term; name a term only if the reader will meet it elsewhere, and then explain it in a few words.

## Faithfulness

- An answer never says more than the article says and never fills a gap: where the article is silent, the answer does not guess.
- Numbers that are in the answer are kept exactly as in the article.
- No promotion and no claims that something is proven.
