## Chapter IV: Governance By Lot: The Sortition Architecture

### Article 4.3: Stratified Selection, Universal Service, and Anti-Bureaucratic Rotation

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**How are the people who serve chosen?**

Names are drawn by lot from public registers of citizens. The draws are open and anyone can verify them. The mix of people chosen is meant to mirror the population by age, gender, and geography.

**Can I refuse if I am summoned?**

The article calls this service a universal prerogative and an inviolable duty. It does not say what happens if someone declines or whether any exceptions exist.

**What happens to my job and my home life while I serve?**

You receive full release from routine labor. Your domestic support is also replaced while you are away.

**Could I serve again right after finishing?**

No. You cannot serve consecutive terms in the same tier. After each term you must wait for a break twice as long as the term you served.

**Who decides what the assembly discusses, and can staff steer it?**

Agenda authority rests with sortition delegates, meaning people chosen by lot. Assembly staff work on strictly temporary, rotating contracts. If staff frame agendas, filter citizens' questions, or covertly influence jurors, that is an impeachable offense.

**How can I get my petition considered?**

Anyone can publish any petition on the open civic register, with no filtering. To reach the agenda, a petition needs co-endorsement from a fixed share of the assembly's constituents. Each person has an equal endorsement allowance that is renewed periodically. Each session has a limited number of agenda slots, filled by public lottery weighted by distinct support.

**Who checks petitions before they go forward?**

A rotating Triage Panel of three (3) citizens drawn by lot. They merge identical requests and check form, never merit. Organic Law fixes the parameters.

> **Warnings (unsupported by the article text):**
> - Answer 1: "Names are drawn by lot from public registers of citizens." (The article says 'universal civic registers'. 'Universal' means the registers cover everyone, not that they are public. Only the lotteries are described as open and publicly verifiable.)
> - Answer 3: "Your domestic support is also replaced while you are away." (The article says domestic support is replaced. It does not say that serving means being away from home.)
