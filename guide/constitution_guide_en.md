## Chapter IV: Governance By Lot: The Sortition Architecture

### Article 4.3: Stratified Selection, Universal Service, and Anti-Bureaucratic Rotation

*not yet reviewed · claude-opus-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**How are people chosen to serve?**

Jurors for every body chosen by lot are drawn from civic registers that include all citizens. The draw uses open lotteries, both digital (cryptographic) and mechanical, and anyone can check them. The selection is balanced so the group matches the population by age, gender, and where people live.

**Can I say no if my name is drawn, and what happens to my job and home duties?**

Serving when you are drawn is described as both a right everyone has and a duty that cannot be set aside. While you serve, you are fully released from your usual work. You also get a replacement for the support you normally provide at home.

**Can I serve again right after my term ends?**

No. You cannot serve two terms in a row at the same level of body. After each term, you must take a break twice as long as the term itself.

**What stops the assembly's staff from quietly running things?**

Staff work only on temporary, rotating contracts. If they shape the agenda, filter citizens' questions, or secretly influence jurors, they can be impeached, meaning formally charged. The power to set the agenda belongs to the delegates chosen by lot.

**How do I get an issue in front of an assembly?**

Anyone can post any petition on the open civic register, and no one filters it. To reach the agenda, it needs endorsements from a set share of the people the assembly serves. Everyone gets the same number of endorsements, renewed from time to time, no matter their work, behavior, or opinions. Each session has a limited number of agenda spots, filled by a public lottery that gives more weight to petitions with more different supporters. A panel of three (3) citizens drawn by lot merges identical requests and checks only the format, not whether a petition is good. The exact figures are set by a separate law, called Organic Law.
