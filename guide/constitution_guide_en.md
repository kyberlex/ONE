# The Constitution in plain questions and answers

A reader's guide, written one article at a time. It explains; it does not replace. Where this guide and the article text differ, the article text prevails.

Entries marked *not yet reviewed* have not been checked by a person. Warnings list statements that an automatic check found unsupported by the article.

## Chapter I: The Preamble & The Invariant Bill Of Rights

### Article 1.1: The Axiom of the Biospheric Commons

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**What counts as the shared commons?**

The article names the atmosphere, the global water cycle, farmable topsoil, the oceans, and the energy that reaches Earth from the sun. It also includes the accumulated scientific and cultural knowledge of humanity. All of this is described as one whole that cannot be divided and that belongs to all living beings.

**Can anyone buy or sell a piece of it for profit?**

No. The article says no part of the commons may be turned into private property for speculation, or used to make money without earning it. It also may not be fenced off as the territory of a government.

**Can I own land, tools, or ideas at all?**

You can hold, use, and benefit from a physical or intellectual resource, but the article calls this a protected right to use rather than full ownership. The right depends on three things: you actively occupy the resource, you care for it in a way that helps it renew, and you keep it intact for later generations.

**What happens if I stop using or looking after something I hold?**

The article says your right to hold it depends on active occupancy, regenerative care, and preserving it for later generations. It does not say what happens if those conditions are not met.

### Article 1.2: Invariant Personal and Bodily Sovereignty

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**What control do I have over my own body and mind?**

The article says every human being has absolute, unconditional control over their own body, physical self and mind.

**What is forbidden under this article?**

It permanently prohibits forced changes to your genes, tracking of your body traits against your will, monitoring of your brain, manipulation through drugs, and physical subjugation. The article calls these violations of personal integrity.

**Which rights does every person keep?**

You keep the right to bodily autonomy, private intimacy and a private inner life. You also keep the right to a compassionate, uncoerced "biological exit", that is, the end of your own life without pressure from anyone.

**Can an emergency or an official order override these protections?**

No. The article says no civic mandate, collective emergency or administrative decree may violate the body or mind of any person.

### Article 1.3: The Universal Baseline of Survival

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Do I have to work to get the basics I need to stay alive?**

No. The article says everyone living here has a right to basic life support that does not depend on a paid job, buying and selling, debt, or agreeing with any set of beliefs. It calls this right a birthright that cannot be given up or taken away.

**What exactly is included in this basic support?**

Six things: drinking water that meets a certified purity standard, enough wholesome food each day, secure shelter that holds up against climate, primary, preventive and emergency healthcare, basic energy for heating, cooling and cooking, and open access to knowledge, culture and basic tools.

**Can I lose this support if I break rules or refuse to do something?**

The article says no group, institution or collective may use your physical survival to shape your behavior, to get work out of you, or to punish you. It does not describe any other rules or exceptions.

**Do I have to prove anything or meet conditions to receive it?**

The article calls the baseline unconditional and says it is separate from work, markets, debt and ideological compliance. It does not say how it is requested or delivered.

**Who provides these things and how are they paid for?**

The article does not say. It sets out what everyone is entitled to, but it does not name who supplies it or how it is funded.

### Article 1.4: Invariant Civil Liberties and Epistemic Freedom

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**What freedoms does this article protect?**

It says every individual and association has unconditional freedom of thought, conscience, speech, peaceful assembly, voluntary association, and cultural expression.

**Can anyone silence peaceful disagreement?**

No. No civic council, assembly, or digital infrastructure may censor peaceful dissent, suppress contrarian inquiry, or require anyone to hold an official belief.

**What is the Heretic's Commons?**

It is a reserve of seven percent (7%) of all shared workshops, computing capacity and research laboratories. It is kept permanently for speculative research that goes against the mainstream view, without needing approval from other researchers. The article says this keeps society able to adapt and stops ideas from going stale.

**What happens if more people apply to use the Commons than there is room for?**

Time is shared out in turns by an open lottery among the applicants. No one is screened for their ideas.

**Is any research off limits, and how is that enforced?**

Yes. Research into synthetic self-replicating lethal pathogens, mass neurological agents or radiological weapons is barred. All machines that build biological sequences must carry publicly checkable, tamper-evident locks that stop them from producing anything on a public hazard list, so the ban needs neither people nor algorithms watching what others think. Disabling or getting around these locks is a crime against the continued life of the biosphere.

### Article 1.5: The Anti-Enclosure Principle

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**What kinds of contracts or titles does this article rule out?**

Any legal title, financial contract, or administrative device that tries to make essential life-support commons private again, bring back absentee landlords, or charge monopoly rents on things people need to live. The article says these are null and void, meaning they have no legal effect.

**What does the article call such attempts?**

It calls them structural aggression against society. The article does not say here what happens to the people who attempt them.

**Can someone own a patent on a scientific discovery?**

No. The article says scientific discoveries are universal public goods and are permanently exempt from monopolistic patents, intellectual property claims, and proprietary restrictions.

**Does the same go for genes, software methods, and tool designs?**

Yes. Biological genetics, computational algorithms, and mechanical tool designs are listed alongside scientific discoveries as universal public goods. They are permanently exempt from patents, intellectual property claims, and proprietary restrictions.

### Article 1.6: Ecological Duty and Intergenerational Stewardship

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**What does this article ask of me?**

If you use the shared natural world, you have a duty to look after it. Human life and activity must stay within what local watersheds (water basins) and the whole living planet can absorb and renew.

**Can I opt out of this duty?**

No. The article calls the duty "inescapable" and says every user of the planetary commons incurs it. It does not describe any exceptions.

**What counts as a crime under this article?**

Three things are named: deliberately poisoning ecosystems, using up aquifers that do not refill (underground water reserves), and destroying habitats in a way that cannot be undone. The article calls them crimes against planetary continuity. It does not say what the penalties are.

**Can nature itself have rights under this article?**

Yes. The article says non-human living systems and regional ecosystems have standing under law in their own right. That means their interests can be defended in legal proceedings.

**Who speaks for nature, and how are they chosen?**

Designated human guardians defend these living systems before civic assemblies. The guardians are drawn by lot, which means chosen by chance. The article does not say how many guardians there are or how long they serve.

### Article 1.7: Primacy and Inviolability of Fundamental Rights

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Which rights does this article protect?**

It protects the rights, freedoms and minimum guarantees set out in Chapter I, which the article calls the Bill of Rights. It describes them as "structural constitutional invariants", meaning they are built into the Constitution and cannot be changed.

**Can a vote, even a very large majority, take these rights away?**

No. No assembly, referendum or qualified supermajority (a majority well above half) may suspend, reduce or give away any provision of the Bill of Rights.

**Can these rights be suspended in an emergency?**

No. The article says a state of exception cannot suspend or reduce them.

**Can I give up one of these rights by signing an agreement?**

Any agreement that goes against this Chapter is void, which means it has no effect.

**What happens to a decree or law that goes against these rights?**

It is void from the start, as if it never had any effect. The article does not say who decides this or how.

## Chapter II: Property Deconstruction & The Jurisprudence Of Usufruct

### Article 2.1: Abolition of Absentee Title and the Deconstruction of Dominium

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Can I still own land or a factory outright?**

No. The article abolishes absolute private ownership, meaning the right to use, hoard, neglect or destroy finite assets however you like. Land, water, farmland, natural areas and industrial machinery can only be held as a conditional right to use them.

**What does the conditional right require of me?**

You must actually use it for something useful, be present, and look after its natural environment.

**What happens if I leave a property empty or just hold it to profit later?**

The article says title cut off from active occupancy and social use does not legally exist. Land, a home or a productive facility that is abandoned or held for passive speculation returns immediately to the local commons.

**What is the local commons?**

The article only says that abandoned or speculative property returns to its jurisdiction. It does not say here how the commons is organized or who decides how the property is then used.

**Where does this rule apply?**

It applies across Open Networked Earth (O.N.E.). The article says the old doctrine of absolute ownership is permanently abolished there.

### Article 2.2: The Tripartite Classification of Goods and Commons

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Can anyone take my personal belongings?**

No. Your clothes, household furnishings, artworks, intimate items, private craft tools and personal computing devices stay yours permanently. No civic assembly can take them, make them collective property or hand them to someone else. The article puts them in the first of three separate groups of things.

**What happens to my tools?**

Tools are split into two kinds. Personal craft gear, such as precision hand tools, trade tools, instruments and workstations you actively use in your craft, stays yours, and others need your consent to use it; spare duplicates do not count, so this protection cannot be used to hoard. Heavy-duty maintenance equipment, such as drills, ladders and pressure washers, is kept for everyone in neighborhood tool libraries.

**How do the neighborhood tool libraries work?**

You borrow items with time-limited booking tokens. If you voluntarily deposit something with the library, the community pays for its upkeep and you get priority booking. If you damage a tool through negligence, you make up for it with guided maintenance hours under the local tool guild.

**Can I lose my home to a bank or a landlord?**

No. Your main home, home plot, family homestead or small workshop, as long as you actually live there and look after it, is held under a permanent agreement outside the market, modeled on Community Land Trusts. It cannot be foreclosed and no rent can be taken from you. The right to live there can pass on to the people who actively live there with you.

**Who runs things like water, forests, hospitals and power grids?**

These are shared commons, held in trust for current and future generations and never divided up. They are run by municipal councils chosen by lottery together with dedicated working guilds. Large industrial plants such as chip factories and chemical plants are run by specialized worker guilds under the oversight of assemblies chosen by lottery, and their wear and environmental cleanup must be covered before any extra output is handed out.

### Article 2.3: Inalienable Housing Security and the Absolute Ban on Eviction

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Can I be evicted from my home?**

Not into homelessness or destitution. The article calls that kind of eviction or displacement a structural crime and says it is unconstitutional.

**Can a landlord rent me a home for money?**

No. The article says residential landlordism, speculative property holding, and monetary rent extraction are legally null and void.

**When might I have to move out of my home?**

The article names three cases: structural hazards, environmental remediation, or domestic crises between people that cannot be resolved. In those cases, moving may be necessary.

**Who decides if there is a dispute about moving me?**

A Local Mediation Panel of three (3) citizens chosen by lot decides the dispute.

**When can a move actually happen?**

Only after the community has secured an equivalent or superior dwelling for you. It must meet the physical, accessibility, and social needs of the people who live there.

### Article 2.4: Stewardship of Arable Soil and Productive Assets

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Can I buy, sell, or rent out farmland, fishing grounds, or factories to make money?**

No. The article says farmable topsoil, marine nurseries (sea areas where young fish grow) and industrial production facilities cannot be bought, sold, mortgaged or leased for monetary profit.

**Who gets to farm the land?**

Farmland is allocated to agroecological collectives, family cultivators, and agrarian trusts. The allocation is based on the nutritional needs of the region and on verified regenerative farming practice.

**How long can I keep the land I farm?**

Cultivators hold the land for many generations, as a right to use it rather than to own it. This lasts as long as soil health, watershed stability, and biodiversity are preserved.

**What happens if I poison the land or stop farming it?**

Persistent ecological poisoning or abandonment of cultivation triggers a review by the Bioregional Citizen Assembly. If a breach of ecological stewardship is verified, the right to farm the land is transferred to willing regenerative custodians.

### Article 2.5: Common Heritage Sanctuaries and Positional Assets

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**What kinds of places does this article protect?**

It covers geographically unique or ecologically sensitive places, such as coastal headlands, pristine riverbanks, alpine crests, and historical sanctuaries. The article calls these "positional assets".

**Can someone buy one of these places and keep it for themselves?**

No. These places can never be enclosed as exclusive private estates.

**Can I build a private home on an unspoiled shoreline?**

No. Permanent private residential construction along pristine shorelines or ecological sanctuaries is prohibited.

**Can anyone stay in these places at all?**

Yes, temporarily. Stays in residential retreats within these sanctuaries are handled through a transparent civic lottery. The article lists three ways in: rotating sabbatical retreats open to all citizens, research fellowships for scientific and artistic contributors, and equitable public visitation.

**Could a powerful family or an official claim one of these places for good?**

No. No lineage or administrative official may establish permanent private dominion over planetary beauty.

### Article 2.6: Adjudication of Usufructuary Disputes and Assembly Thresholds

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Who decides on changes to neighborhood land use?**

A Neighborhood Sortition Council of fifteen (15) citizens chosen by lot decides, in an open discussion. A change needs a sixty percent (60%) majority.

**Who decides on industrial zoning and watershed easements for a whole region?**

A Bioregional Citizen Assembly of one hundred and one (101) delegates decides, in an open discussion. A change needs a sixty percent (60%) majority.

**Can my right to use and hold my land or home be taken away or changed for a public project?**

Yes, but only through an extraordinary decree for essential public infrastructure. It needs seventy-five percent (75%) of the seated assembly.

**What must I be given if my possessory right is revoked or modified this way?**

You must be given equivalent housing beforehand, along with full physical restitution. The Local Mediation Panel must certify this unanimously.

**What can I do if the Local Mediation Panel refuses to certify?**

The refusal can be appealed to what the article calls a cross-watershed blind panel of fifteen (15) citizens. The article does not say more about how that panel works.

## Chapter III: Biophysical Reality & Thermodynamic Resource Accounting

### Article 3.1: The Thermodynamic Boundary and Demonetization of the Commons

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**What is this article about, in plain terms?**

It says that all economic planning, industry and sharing out of resources across Open Networked Earth (O.N.E.) must follow the physical laws of energy and the balance of materials in nature. Money-based claims lose their legal force over shared natural resources.

**What happens to money, debt and speculation?**

Money that is not backed by anything physical (fiat money), debt that compounds over time and speculative pricing lose their legal standing as claims on the shared natural world. The article does not say what happens to them outside that area.

**What is used to measure and count resources instead?**

Four things: usable energy in kilowatt-hours (kWh), dry material weight in kilograms (kg), clean water in litres (L), and the time it takes for natural or reusable cycles to renew themselves.

**Is there a limit on how much a community can use?**

Yes. No group of people may plan for, take, or use more than the verified rate at which their local watersheds and the biosphere replenish. No production may be authorized beyond local replacement limits.

**What must a community show about its basic needs?**

Every year a public audit must show that all the sectors that supply Tier 1 needs, the basic survival needs set out in Article 3.3, including shelter and healthcare, can meet the full demand using only local resources and equipment already in place. Outside inputs must be zero, and a reserve margin must be published.

### Article 3.2: Absolute Prohibition of Usury, Speculation, and Debt Compounding

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**What kinds of contracts does this article ban?**

It bans any contract, algorithm, or ledger that requires exponential compound interest, debt bondage, or financial derivative speculation. Such agreements are unconstitutional, void, and cannot be enforced.

**What is credit for, under this article?**

Credit exists only as the planned administrative allocation of physical energy, raw materials, and tool time. These resources must go to verified civic infrastructure, communal maintenance, or ecological restoration.

**Can a debt grow over time?**

No. The article says no debt may compound.

**Can I be asked to put up shared land, nature, or my home as security for a loan?**

No. No commons, ecosystem, or dwelling may be pledged as commercial collateral.

**What happens to me if a project I was involved in fails?**

A project failure must never leave you destitute or in forced labor. It also must never cost you your usufruct, the conditional right to hold and use a home, land or tools (Article 1.1).

### Article 3.3: The Three-Tier Democratic Hierarchy of Needs

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**What comes first when resources are handed out?**

Survival basics, called Tier 1: verified drinking water, wholesome daily food, secure housing, heating and cooling at home, and preventive and emergency healthcare. The article calls this priority absolute and non-negotiable. Upkeep of shared infrastructure (Tier 2) comes second, and personal and creative projects (Tier 3) last.

**What is the shared infrastructure that gets kept up second?**

Tier 2 is the upkeep of water treatment plants, small local power grids, public transit routes, open communication networks, hospitals, and repair workshops with fabrication tools (fab-labs).

**How do I get time on a shared machine for art, craft, or science projects?**

Art, craft and science fall under Tier 3, which is served only once Tiers 1 and 2 are secured. Spare resources are shared out through a way of signaling preferences that the article calls quadratic preference signaling, and through shared sign-up schedules for workshop machines, with no central office and no prices. A popular machine slot cannot be passed on, the same people cannot keep taking it over, and an unused slot goes after a short grace period to people on standby drawn by lot.

**Can I keep my designs private if I use a shared machine?**

No. Everything run on shared machines, including the machine instructions and design files, is automatically published to the open shared network (Article 1.4). The article says this prevents secret commercial use.

**What is the Tier 2-A buffer for?**

It protects basic heavy industry, such as metal smelting, silicon purification, chip-making and heavy tooling, which the article treats as shared capital that cannot be split up. These plants do not compete with Tier 3 projects for resources, and their replacement and wear reserves must be restored before any surplus goes to personal fabrication.

### Article 3.4: Bioregional Carrying Capacity, Telemetry, and Analog Ground-Truthing

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**What limits how much people can take from the land or make in a watershed?**

Human extraction and manufacturing in each watershed (water basin) are limited by what the article calls its Ecological Carrying Capacity. The article does not say how that limit is calculated.

**What is measured, and who can see the results?**

Continuous sensors measure non-personal physical variables: how fast underground water refills, river flow, carbon in the soil, growth of plant and animal matter, load on the power grid, and heat given off. Anyone can see the readings.

**Can these sensors be used to watch me?**

No. The sensors are strictly confined to non-living and large-scale environmental variables. Algorithmic surveillance, biometric tracking, or profiling of an individual household is called an absolute constitutional crime.

**How do I know the sensors can be trusted?**

The sensor hardware must use open-hardware controllers whose built-in software can be checked exactly, bit for bit. Closed, secret code and sealed-off chip areas that cannot be inspected are not allowed.

**What happens if the sensors or the digital models drift away from reality?**

Neighborhood Councils regularly send out squads of ordinary citizens with manual, non-digital instruments to check the readings. Duplicate sealed samples are tested independently by laboratories drawn by lot from watersheds that do not border each other, so the digital records stay tied to physical reality.

### Article 3.5: Strategic Physical Reserves and Non-Linear Resilience

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**What is a Strategic Physical Reserve?**

It is a stock of physical goods and capacity that each bioregional federation keeps in case of disruptions, droughts, solar storms, or epidemics. The aim is to keep basic production going when things go wrong.

**What is kept in the reserves?**

Storable stocks such as grains, staples, heirloom seeds, critical pharmaceuticals, and energy storage. They also include spare parts and extra capacity for goods that cannot be stored. These stocks are spread out rather than held in one place.

**How long should the reserves last?**

They are meant to keep Tier 1 output, the basic survival needs (Article 3.3), going for 12 to 24 months without outside inputs.

**How is it decided what the reserves must cover?**

Reserves follow the gaps found by the yearly audit of Article 3.1.6, which checks whether basic needs can be met from local resources alone. The article calls it the Autarkic Closure audit.

**Can the reserves be sold or used for other purposes?**

No. They cannot be sold off for speculative trade, exported for outside money, or diverted to non-essential uses.

### Article 3.6: Auditing, Assembly Parity, and Decision Thresholds

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Who can see the records of how resources and energy are used?**

Anyone can. The ledgers and balance sheets are public and written in accessible language. They are reviewed continuously by groups of citizens chosen by lottery.

**Who checks the neighborhood utilities, and who oversees the larger regional energy and reserves?**

Neighborhood utility monitoring is handled by Neighborhood Sortition Councils of fifteen (15) citizens. Regional energy distribution and oversight of strategic reserves are handled by Bioregional Citizen Assemblies of one hundred and one (101) delegates. Sortition means members are chosen by lottery.

**How many votes does it take to change the basic survival allocations?**

It takes a seventy-five percent (75%) supermajority to change, reduce or recalculate the basic survival allowance per person (Tier 1).

**What majority is needed for energy budgets and discretionary allocations?**

A 60% qualified majority. This covers five-year bioregional infrastructure energy budgets and Tier 3 discretionary allocations.

**Can reserves be used quickly in an emergency, and is there a check on that?**

Yes. During a certified natural disaster or ecological crisis, a 50%+1 simple majority is enough to mobilize the Strategic Physical Reserves immediately. A review is mandatory after thirty (30) days.

## Chapter IV: Governance By Lot: The Sortition Architecture

### Article 4.1: The Principle of the Lot (Demarchy) and Abolition of Oligarchy

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Who has the power to make laws and decide big questions under this article?**

Only assemblies of citizens chosen by lottery. The lottery is set up statistically so that the group reflects the population. All lawmaking, debating and constitutional power comes from these assemblies.

**Does this mean there are elections and political parties?**

No. The article abolishes electoral party politics, campaign financing, corporate lobbying and permanent parliamentary classes. It says they are abolished because they let a small group capture power.

**Can I still join a group or campaign for a cause?**

Yes. You remain free to associate and advocate, as Article 1.4 provides. But no group may finance, nominate, instruct or reward the citizens chosen by lottery; any such act is void, and the article calls it a structural assault on the civic commons.

### Article 4.2: Tiered Assembly Architecture and Invariant Odd Parity

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Why does every group have an odd number of members?**

The article says odd numbers are used to prevent ties and deadlock. Each chamber is kept at an odd size without exception.

**What groups are there, and how big is each one?**

Five. A Local Mediation Panel has 3 citizens, a Neighborhood Sortition Council 15, a Bioregional Citizen Assembly 101 delegates, a Continental Governance Chamber 301 citizens, and the Global Commons Assembly 1,001 citizens. Each covers a larger area than the one before.

**How long does someone serve?**

It depends on the group: three months on a Local Mediation Panel, six months on a Neighborhood Council, one year in a Bioregional Assembly, eighteen months in a Continental Chamber and two years in the Global Assembly. In the three largest groups the terms are staggered, so members do not all change at once.

**What happens in a very small settlement?**

If a settlement has fewer than 50 adult residents, its 3-person local panel also runs the settlement's administration. First-degree relatives and domestic partners may not serve on the panel at the same time. When the settlement reaches 50 adult residents, a 15-citizen neighborhood council is set up automatically.

**Can I appeal a ruling in a very small settlement?**

Yes. Every party to a ruling can appeal to a cross-watershed blind panel of 15 citizens, and appeals against the same ruling are joined. A decision that needs 75% and that the three-person panel cannot agree on unanimously goes to a vote of all adult residents, again needing 75%.

### Article 4.3: Stratified Selection, Universal Service, and Anti-Bureaucratic Rotation

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**How are the people who serve chosen?**

By lottery from civic registers that include everyone. The draws use cryptographic and mechanical methods and anyone can check them. The people drawn are balanced to mirror the population by age, gender and place.

**Can I refuse if I am picked, and who does my work meanwhile?**

The article calls this service both a right of everyone and a duty that cannot be set aside; it does not describe a way to decline. If you are called, you are fully released from your routine work, and your domestic support is replaced. The article does not say who provides that replacement.

**Could I serve again right after finishing?**

No, not at the same level of assembly. After each term you must take a break twice as long as the term itself.

**Can the assembly's staff steer what is discussed?**

No. Staff work only on temporary, rotating contracts, and the power to set the agenda belongs to the members chosen by lot. Framing agendas, filtering citizens' questions or secretly influencing members is an "impeachable offense", meaning staff can be formally charged for it.

**How can I get my petition considered?**

Anyone can publish any petition on the open civic register, unfiltered. To reach the agenda it needs endorsements from a fixed share of the people the assembly serves, and everyone gets the same endorsement allowance, renewed regularly and never tied to work, behavior or opinions. Agenda slots are limited and filled by public lottery, weighted by how many different people support each petition; a rotating panel of three (3) citizens drawn by lot merges identical requests and checks form, never merit.

### Article 4.4: Adversarial Expert Testimony and Intelligible Decision-Making

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Who actually decides on a proposal, the jurors or the experts?**

The jurors, the citizens chosen by lot, deliberate and decide; the article calls them the ethical conscience of the commons. Technical advisors only provide evidence and do not vote.

**What happens when a proposal is complex?**

Two independent rival expert panels cross-examine each other's assumptions and biases in open session. Neither panel may be funded by corporate grants or be in debt.

**Will I be able to understand what the experts say?**

That is the aim. Experts must translate their models into plain-language consequences, trade-offs, and failure scenarios. The burden of doing so is on them.

**What if the expert panels just create confusion or stall on purpose?**

Then the assembly withholds approval, applying the Precautionary Principle: when in doubt, decide in favor of nature and life. Deadlocks that persist go to a panel of working scientists drawn by lot from watersheds that do not border each other.

**Could this caution stop routine upkeep or life-saving services?**

No. Precautionary suspension applies only to novel structural interventions. It may never interrupt certified routine maintenance or critical life support.

### Article 4.5: Deliberative Protocols and Graduated Voting Thresholds

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**How many votes does it take to pass something in a deliberative assembly?**

It depends on the subject. There are three levels: 75%, 60%, and 50%+1. Assemblies vote only through these levels.

**What needs the highest bar, 75%?**

Changes to the structure of this Constitution, expansions of the basic rights (which can only be expanded, never reduced, Article 1.7), declarations that something can no longer be bought or sold, and changes to the survival baseline.

**What needs 60%?**

Multi-year bioregional development plans, foundational resource allocations, inter-watershed treaties, and general regulatory statutes.

**When is a simple majority enough, and what happens to emergency decisions made that way?**

A simple majority (50%+1) is allowed only for routine administrative scheduling, procedural assembly motions, and immediate emergency disaster responses. An emergency decree passed this way ends automatically after thirty (30) days, and this cannot be extended. To continue, it must be ratified at the sixty percent (60%) level.

**Are percentages counted from the people present or from everyone in the assembly?**

They are counted from all seated members, not just those present. Each threshold is the smallest whole number of votes equal to or above that share. For example, in an assembly of 15 you need 9 votes for 60% and 12 for 75%. In an assembly of 101 you need 61 and 76.

### Article 4.6: Constitutional Review (*Custodes Constitutionis*)

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Who can ask for a law or decision to be checked against the Constitution?**

Any citizen, any assembly, or any Guardian of the Biosphere (the people who speak for nature, Article 10.2) can ask. The request can target any law, decree, algorithm or Organic Law said to violate the Constitution.

**Who decides whether an act breaks the Constitution?**

A jury of 101 citizens chosen by lot, formed for that one case, from watersheds not involved in the dispute. It deliberates under the rules of Article 4.4.

**How many jurors must agree to cancel an act?**

Sixty percent (60%) must agree for the act to be voided. When a question about the basic rights of Chapter I is in doubt, the jury decides in favor of the person.

**Can I appeal if I disagree with the jury's decision?**

Yes. Each party may appeal once. The appeal goes to a fresh jury of 301 citizens.

**Can a review jury rewrite the law it strikes down?**

No. A review jury can only declare that an act conforms to the Constitution or that it is void. It may not draft or amend law.

Large computing jobs, such as machine learning and heavy simulations, are slowed or paused instantly when renewable power falls short. This protects the basic survival needs (Tier 1, Article 3.3).

High-grade waste heat from large computing clusters and heat-producing chemical processes is piped into municipal district heating networks and into greenhouses.

Life-critical municipal systems must have physical switches that can instantly cut them off from outside networks during a cyber attack. ## Chapter VII: Open Compiler Integrity & The Democratization Of Code

Operating systems, compilers, cryptographic libraries, database structures, and the firmware inside Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) or microprocessors. All of these must be in the public domain.

Operating system kernels, cryptographic libraries, and firmware for industrial controllers. They must go through Deterministic Diverse Double-Compilation, which means being built with several functionally independent, open-source toolchains and on different processor designs.

Public repositories must keep decentralized build records that can be audited with cryptography. A program's compiled cryptographic hash, a kind of digital fingerprint, must be reproducible from the public source by any citizen node.

At this third stage, sensor and data evidence is audited independently by a facility drawn by lot from a watershed that is not next to the one involved. The evidence is admitted only if it can be reproduced under open review.

The article uses the term without defining it. In general it means the right to use something without owning it. The article does not say who may use these properties or on what terms.

Corporate patents, trade secrets, and monopoly intellectual property claims over essential medicines, renewable energy schematics, and mechanical tools are declared public usufruct. They are immediately published to open-source hardware repositories.

### Article 9.3: The External Trade Interface (ETI) and Demonetization Buffer

**What is the External Trade Interface?**

It is the channel that bioregional federations use to get essential goods from outside O.N.E. These goods are industrial commodities that are not available locally, specialized microelectronics, and advanced medicines. They come from what the article calls legacy jurisdictions.

**Does money get used inside the O.N.E. commons?**

No. The article says that inside the commons there is no fiat currency, no speculative debt, and no market-based price mechanisms. The trade interface exists so that buying from outside does not bring monetary inequality back in.

**What kind of sales to the outside world does the article mention?**

It gives two examples: exporting surplus renewable electricity and offering specialized engineering consultations. These outward commercial dealings are kept inside the External Trade Interface and separate from the commons.

**What happens to money that comes in from outside sales?**

Under the Twenty-Four-Hour Conversion Rule, all external fiat revenue must be committed within twenty-four (24) hours. It goes to irrevocable physical procurement contracts, dedicated escrow, or direct delivery of non-depreciating assets, strategic grain reserves, or specialized raw materials.

**Why is the money committed so quickly?**

The article says the rule prevents financial speculation and sovereign asset freezing. It does not explain further how the rule does this.

### Article 9.4: Total Asymmetric Dissuasion, Defection Bounties, and Industrial Denial

**Does O.N.E. have an army?**

No. The article rejects standing armies sent abroad, forced military service for offensive purposes, and hired mercenary forces. Defence instead relies on civil resistance, fortified spaces, cutting off an attacker's supplies, and efforts to change enemy minds.

**How are food, energy, and medical supplies protected from attack?**

They are spread out and stored in reinforced underground storage units in every micro-watershed. The article says this removes any single target for air bombing, drone strikes, or naval blockades.

**What happens to an enemy soldier who stops fighting?**

Any soldier, officer, or contractor who puts down working weapons, refuses offensive orders, or sabotages hostile operations is guaranteed immediate political asylum. They also get lifelong Tier 1 biological security, a home they can keep without encumbrance, and cancellation of external debts for themselves and their family.

**What becomes of weapons that are surrendered or captured?**

All surrendered, captured, or decommissioned weapons, armored vehicle bodies, and ammunition in the territory are permanently taken apart and melted down in public foundries. The metal is turned into wind turbine shafts, farm tools, and building frames.

**What if an invader gets past the outer defences and occupies the territory?**

Randomly selected authorities carry out a distributed key sever, using keys protected by Threshold Multi-Party Computation. This permanently switches off high-level industrial and computing functions, so the invader gets no supply or financial value from them. Basic household water and heating are not cut off.

### Article 9.5: Universal Debt Jubilees and Insolvent Asset Transformation

**What happens to factories, distribution hubs, and utility grids that have failed or been abandoned?**

Defaulted industrial production centers, bankrupt distribution hubs, and abandoned utility grids are expropriated, which means taken over. They go into municipal commons trusts. The article does not say how the taking is carried out or whether the former owners are paid.

Compound-interest debts, predatory residential mortgages, and financial liens held by legacy institutions against personal dwellings and life-support infrastructure are abolished. The article calls this a Debt Jubilee, meaning a general cancellation of debts. It describes the cancellation as comprehensive and unencumbered.

**Does this cancel every debt I owe?**

The article names specific kinds: compound-interest debts, predatory residential mortgages, and liens against personal dwellings and life-support infrastructure held by legacy institutions. It does not mention other kinds of debt, so it does not say whether they are affected.

They are re-engineered with open-source programmable logic controllers (the computer units that run machinery) and modular machine tools. The uses named are recycling of metals in a circular way, repair of farm machinery, and local civic utility.

**Who owns the reclaimed facilities?**

The article says the defaulted centers, hubs, and grids are expropriated into municipal commons trusts. It does not explain how these trusts are run or who takes part in them.

Annual budgets for outside trade need a 60% qualified majority. So do regional mutual-aid agreements with neighboring areas that are also in transition.

A simple majority, meaning 50% plus one, is allowed only for defensive non-lethal interdiction and for quick supply adjustments during a serious outside embargo. Either action must then go through a mandatory legislative review within thirty (30) days.

No. The article names these actions and the vote each one needs, but it does not explain how they are carried out.

## Chapter X: Bioregional Watershed Federation & The Planetary Covenant

### Article 10.1: The Watershed as Primary Sovereign Unit and Bioregional Subsidiarity

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**What happens to national borders under this article?**

The article says Westphalian frontiers, colonial borders, and sovereign territorial enclosures are permanently abolished. It does not say what replaces them beyond the watershed unit described next.

**What is the basic unit of political organization?**

It is the natural Bioregional Watershed. A watershed is bounded by hydrological catchments (areas where water drains), aquifers (underground water stores), and coastal biomes (coastal natural habitats).

**Who makes decisions, and at what level?**

Decisions are made at the lowest ecological scale possible. The article gives a range from neighborhood micro-basins up to valley councils.

**Can a higher assembly overrule my local community?**

Generally no. Higher-tier assemblies have no authority to override local autonomy. The article lists three exceptions: halting pollution that is exported to others, protecting shared aquifers, and defending invariant rights.

**What are invariant rights?**

The article does not say. It only names them as one of the three cases where a higher assembly may step in.

### Article 10.2: Ecological Personhood and Interspecies Guardianship (*Jus Biosphaerae*)

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Does a river or a forest have legal rights under this article?**

Yes. Major river systems, ancient forests, coastal wetlands, and aquifers are treated as legal persons. They hold the right to exist, flow, and regenerate, and that right cannot be given away.

**Can a natural biome be bought, rented, or sold?**

No. The article says natural biomes cannot be owned, leased, or commodified, which means treated as goods to be traded.

**Who speaks for a river or forest when decisions are made?**

Guardians of the Biosphere do. They are made up of citizens chosen by lot and rotating field naturalists. They represent non-human biomes and future generations in all assemblies.

**Can the Guardians actually stop a project?**

They have standing to enjoin projects that threaten ecological continuity. In plain words, they can bring a case to ask that such a project be stopped. The article does not say how that process works.

### Article 10.3: Physical Inter-Basin Reciprocity and Anti-Cartel Pacts

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**How do different regions trade with each other under this article?**

They trade through bilateral physical resource compacts, which are agreements between two parties. These operate without financial speculation, debt instruments, or currency exchange.

Basins exchange physical exergy (usable energy), refined metals, and specialized equipment directly. They record these exchanges on open thermodynamic ledgers.

Dense urban areas act as specialized metabolic organs under bilateral compacts. They trade advanced industrial outputs and scientific research for food and energy surpluses.

It triggers automatic Systemic Decoupling, called Disiunctio Reticularis. Inter-basin grid interties, transit corridors, and advanced medical buffers are suspended immediately.

**How long does the suspension last?**

It lasts until reciprocity is restored. The article does not say who decides that reciprocity is restored or how it is done.

### Article 10.4: Thermodynamic Freedom of Movement and Non-Coercive Carrying Capacity

*not yet reviewed · claude-sonnet-5-5 · 2026-11-16*

**Can I travel anywhere I like?**

The article says every human being has a right to travel, visit, and seek refuge across the shared planet. Passports, border fences, visa checkpoints, and armed border patrols are barred.

**Can I settle permanently wherever I choose?**

Not always. Permanent settlement in a region depends on a status that measures how many people its environment can support: Green, Amber, or Red. The article calls this measure Ecological Carrying Capacity.

**What do the Green, Amber, and Red statuses mean for someone who wants to settle?**

Under Green, the region takes in new residents freely and you get immediate residence and enrollment in the civic assembly. Under Amber, permanent places go first to family reunification, specialized labor requests, and voluntary infrastructure expansion. Under Red, new permanent residence is paused until the region's capacity grows.

**Can I be turned away or deported if a region is at Red status?**

No. The article says no one may face armed pushback, physical blocking, or forced deportation. Even under Red, all travelers keep access to hospitality pavilions, clean water, food, emergency medical care, and safe transit.

**What happens when too many people arrive in a Red-status region?**

Neighboring Green and Amber regions must set up mutual-aid corridors, sending water desalination units, portable power grids, and mobile medical hubs. If arrivals exceed what local hospitality services can sustain, the Continental Chamber triggers a protocol that moves arriving people by high-speed rail and ship across the crowded area to reception points in nearby Green and Amber regions.

### Article 10.5: Planetary Commons Stewardship and Solar Flux Regulation

**What does this article treat as shared by everyone?**

The global atmosphere, the oceans held in common, the frozen regions of the planet, and the electromagnetic spectrum. The article calls them the indivisible life-support heritage of all living organisms.

**Can anyone spray chemicals into the sky or the sea to cool the planet?**

No. Releasing aerosols into the atmosphere, spreading sulfur in the stratosphere, and adding chemicals to the ocean to fertilize it are permanently prohibited. The article describes them as existential ecocidal hazards. It also bans them when done unilaterally.

**Is any method of controlling sunlight allowed?**

Yes, one kind. A system that does not use chemicals, can be reversed, and works by regulating light from deep space is recognized as a trust for the Sol Commons under Article 11.1. It is meant to counter runaway climate tipping points without chemical fallout.

**Who has to approve such a system before it is put in place?**

Two groups. First, 75% of the Global Commons Assembly, which has 1,001 delegates, must approve it. Second, a majority of the Continental Governance Chambers must agree, and each chamber must do so by a 60% qualified majority.

**What happens if the system loses its authorization or control is lost?**

It must fail safe. It disperses gradually over a multi-year interval that Organic Law sets, and never abruptly. The article says this prevents administrative capture and termination shock. The article does not say how long that interval is.

There are five layers. Local Mediation Panels have 3 citizens, Neighborhood Sortition Councils have 15, Bioregional Citizen Assemblies have 101 delegates, Continental Governance Chambers have 301, and the Global Commons Assembly has 1,001. Each size is an odd number.

It is a pledge by all federated bioregions to support each other, keep peace, and share care for the Earth and all its descendants. The article does not say how the pledge is enforced.

## Chapter XI: The Celestial Commons & Interplanetary Federation

### Article 11.1: The Sol Commons and Abolition of Celestial Dominium (*Patrimonium Solis*)

**What counts as the shared celestial commons?**

Outer space, celestial bodies, asteroids, comets, the Lagrange equilibrium points (L1 to L5) and orbital trajectories. The article treats them together as one indivisible commons, called Patrimonium Solis.

Deep-space solar flux regulation (Article 10.5.3) is classified as a Sol Commons trust. It cannot be enclosed for commercial use or used as a weapon.

Not outright. Extracted abiotic minerals and volatiles are held only as a right to use them, and only while you actively steward them. You must also publish structural assays and process tooling into the Open Commons immediately.

The article names an orbital cylinder, a lunar lava tube, a Martian regolith vault (a sealed space built in Mars's loose surface soil), and the hull of a spacecraft that travels a repeating route between worlds.

The article says the stock of water, nitrogen, carbon and phosphorus is closed and cannot be altered. It calls these stocks community trusts, and it describes the balance of materials as an absolute ceiling.

The article lists four measures: how efficiently resources are recirculated, how fast volatile substances leak out, how well the structure holds its pressure, and how much heat the radiators can shed, in watts per square metre.

No. Habitation permits and expansions of space must strictly conform to verifiable biophysical equilibrium, meaning a balance that can be shown to hold. The article does not say who checks this or how.

In every pressurized habitat and transit vessel, you have a right to breathable air at safe pressures, drinkable water, protection from radiation, and enough heat to stay safe. The article calls these universal survival rights that cannot be set aside.

If you are a non-reciprocal inhabitant, you may face a temporary suspension of optional Tier 3 tooling or computational bandwidth. The article does not define Tier 3 here. You may never be expelled into vacuum, confined in a way that restricts your basic physical needs, or have your air rationed.

Ratifications and dispute arbitrations that involve several regions are synchronized over delay-tolerant mesh networks. They use cryptographic append-only ledgers, which are records that can only have entries added.

It maintains spread-out, dual-use interception equipment instead of standing military forces. In peacetime this equipment is used for civilian propulsion and for removing orbital debris.

No. Every directed-energy emitter has a hardware interlock, independent of software, that cuts its power whenever it is aimed at an inhabited body or habitat.

No one decides in the moment. The last stage of interception runs on pre-compiled, fixed firmware, which assemblies chosen by lottery (sortition) audit in advance. All planetary trajectories are verified by optical-radar networks that use Byzantine Fault Tolerant (BFT) methods. The article does not explain how these methods work.

They are handed over to cycler foundries and remelted into common habitat rings and thermal radiators. The article calls this principle "Swords into Plowshares."
