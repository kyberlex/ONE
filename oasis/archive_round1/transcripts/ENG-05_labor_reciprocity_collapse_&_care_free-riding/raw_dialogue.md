### SECTION 1: RESOLUTION OF THE ALLEGED SANCTION/EVICTION CONTRADICTION
**(Refuting Interrogation Vector 1: Art. 5.2.2 vs. Art. 2.3.1 & Art. 2.3.4)**

The adversary claims that the fourth-stage sanction of Article 5.2.2 (relocation to "baseline subsistence accommodations") is paralyzed because Article 2.3.4 mandates that replacement housing be "equivalent or superior," while Article 2.3.1 brands displacement a "structural crime." This is a false legal antinomy.

#### 1. The Usufructuary Boundary Condition of Tenure
Under the Jurisprudence of Usufruct, title divorced from active utility and stewardship is legally non-existent (Art. 2.1.2, Art. 2.1.3). Usufruct is explicitly defined as:
> *"the protected right to hold, utilize, and benefit from a resource conditional upon active occupancy, regenerative care, and preservation of its integrity for succeeding generations."* (Art. 1.1.3).

A residential dwelling linked to communal greywater networks, central HVAC loops, or municipal microgrids consumes real, ongoing Tier 2 physical maintenance labor. When an inhabitant systematically refuses civic reciprocity shifts (Art. 5.1.1, Art. 5.2.2), they cease to fulfill the regenerative conditions of possessory usufruct over *that specific high-maintenance asset*. They are attempting to unilaterally convert federated commons usufruct into passive, unearned extractive *dominium*, which is abolished *ab initio* (Art. 2.1.1).

#### 2. Distinct Jurisprudential Spheres: Article 2.3 vs. Article 5.2.2
The textual scope of Article 2.3.4 is strictly tethered to the circumstances enumerated in Article 2.3.3:
> *"Where structural hazards, environmental remediation, or irreconcilable interpersonal domestic crises necessitate physical relocation..."* (Art. 2.3.3).

In those three non-fault instances, the occupant has committed no usufructuary breach; therefore, equity requires an "equivalent or superior dwelling satisfying physiological, accessibility, and social requirements" (Art. 2.3.4) to prevent administrative revictimization.

Conversely, Article 5.2.2 addresses an affirmative breach of the reciprocity covenant:
> *"...applied specifically where the former dwelling incurs communal maintenance loads the individual refuses to reciprocate."* (Art. 5.2.2).

#### 3. Defining "Baseline Subsistence Accommodation" Without Homelessness
Article 2.3.1 strictly bars eviction into **homelessness or destitution**. Relocation under Article 5.2.2 does not cast the individual into homelessness or destitution. It transitions them into an autonomous, low-maintenance residential unit that:
* Fully secures every element of the Invariant Universal Baseline of Survival (Art. 1.3.2): potable water, wholesome caloric nutrition, climate-resilient residential shelter, domestic heating/cooling, and comprehensive primary healthcare.
* Decouples the inhabitant from municipal systems requiring external, unreciprocated communal maintenance labor (e.g., transitioning from a complex hydronic multi-family dwelling requiring sewer de-sludging shifts to an autonomous, dry-sanitation, passive-solar residential unit engineered under the Black-Sky Protocol of Art. 6.5).

This satisfies Article 2.3.4’s physiological and accessibility baselines under Tier 1, while perfectly terminating the defector's unearned extraction of other citizens' physical labor. It is a legitimate, constitutional reallocation of usufructuary assets, not an eviction into destitution.
