## **4. Proposal on Ceilings** *(for the maintainer; nothing here is in force)*

**Decided 2026-11-20:** the ceiling of Chapter I is raised. **Not decided:** to what.

1. **Chapter I.** Its ceiling should be set once the chapter is fully drafted, not before. What is certain today is 1,436 words (or 1,379), and at least six further sentences are owed. A number chosen now would be a guess. *Proposal:* suspend the 1,200 ceiling for Chapter I until the drafts of section 3 exist; then set the ceiling at the counted length plus a small margin, by one decision. If a number is wanted now as a working limit, **1,600** covers what is drafted and leaves about 160 words for what is not; it is a judgment, not a derivation.
2. **Chapter IV.** A decision is owed whatever happens to Chapter I: 2,183 against 2,150, and 2,231 if Y-12 and the Y-3 bound are accepted. It was postponed until Y-3 and Y-12 are decided (board, section B).
3. **The whole.** 11,500 is already passed. A new total cannot be derived before the twelve dockets are drafted. *Proposal:* keep the rule that no docket raises a ceiling, suspend the figure of 11,500, and require every draft from now on to state the count of its chapter and of the whole, so that the next recount is a sum and not a search.
4. **The other chapters.** Seven are claimed by more dockets than they have room for (consolidation, section 5). The same sequence applies: draft, count, then decide, chapter by chapter.

**What this costs, stated plainly.** The ceilings were adopted to stop the Constitution from growing docket by docket; the red team of the rule foresaw this as "ceiling creep" (Y-16, T-3). Suspending figures until drafting ends removes the brake for a time. The brake that remains is instruction 8 and the placement test: detail goes to Organic Law, and only rights, prohibitions, powers, thresholds and bounds stay. Each draft should show that it applied the test.
