# Experts Drawn by Lot: a Proposal for Citizens' Assemblies

*A plain-language summary of one proposal under test. About ten minutes to read.*

## What this is

O.N.E. (Open Networked Earth) is an open, non-commercial draft constitution in which decisions are made by assemblies of ordinary citizens picked by lot. This page describes one mechanism only: how those assemblies get expert advice. Everything here is a proposal under test. It is not part of the constitution text, and every number on this page is provisional.

## The problem

Citizens deciding whether to rebuild a levee, or how to upgrade a water treatment plant, need expert advice. Both usual formats have known weaknesses.

The first is two opposing expert teams. This rewards the better talker, not the better evidence. A fringe view gets half the floor, and a side that wants to block a decision can win by spreading confusion.

The second is experts chosen by the organizers. Whoever chooses them frames the question, and a professional body that controls who counts as an expert can fill both "opposing" teams with people who share the same premises. The same experts also come back case after case and learn how to steer assemblies.

## The proposal, step by step

1. **One public register.** One register of practitioners serves every assembly, with the same entry rules everywhere.

2. **Anyone who meets the written criteria can join.** The criteria are written in advance and are countable facts, never judgments of quality. "Ten years of documented practice" would qualify; "a significant contribution to the field" would not. No real criteria have been drafted yet. No professional body, guild or school decides who gets in.

3. **Three kinds of proof, on equal footing.** A work that can be checked, such as an open design with its history or an installation that exists. A signed statement from the person or community that received the work, never from fellow experts. A diploma confirmed by the school that issued it. None is required, and any one is enough. A well digger with twenty working wells and no diploma has a route in.

4. **A machine helps, but never says no.** An open software model reads each application and answers only "meets the criteria" or "needs checking", never "no". "Needs checking" goes to a jury of citizens picked by lot from outside the field, who decide. One in five "meets" answers is re-checked by such a jury. A new entry waits six months before it can be picked, and it can never be picked for a proposal published before it applied.

5. **Experts are picked by lot for one case.** When an assembly faces a complex proposal, the citizens name the fields involved, and a panel is drawn from the register for that case only. The draw leaves out anyone with a stake in the proposal, keeps at least one seat each for people from the area and from outside it (about one third local), and never seats two people whose proof comes from the same school or the same source.

6. **One written report, disagreements printed at equal length.** The panel writes one plain-language report: what the proposal and doing nothing would each lead to, what the experts agree on, where they differ, and what observation would change each one's mind. Each expert signs. A dissent gets the same length and place as the majority text.

7. **Citizens lead and decide alone.** Supporters and opponents then speak as declared interested parties. The citizens ask the questions and may call more witnesses. They deliberate without experts or parties in the room. Doubt alone never blocks a decision: to stop a new project, the record must show a specific, plausible way it could cause irreversible harm.

8. **The panel dissolves.** After the report, the panel ends. Serving gives no title or office, and each member sits out a rest period in that field before being picked again. A later panel on the same matter states what it confirms or revises in the earlier report.

## How privacy is handled

A list of experts in contested fields is also a target list, so the register hides people by default.

- **Pseudonyms by default.** Every entry is under a pseudonym. A real name appears only if the person chooses. A panel member gets a different pseudonym for each case.
- **Proofs are sealed.** Only the citizen juries picked by lot see the evidence. The public sees "criteria met" and overall statistics. Anyone may still ask a jury to re-check an entry for a stated reason, such as "this work does not exist".
- **The right to leave.** A person may withdraw at any time; they are never picked again and their evidence is deleted. Signed reports stay public under the pseudonym.
- **Protection of clients and patients.** A signed statement from someone who received the work states only dates, kind of work and quantities, never a diagnosis or personal condition. The signer can stay unnamed once proven to be a real person, and clients called as witnesses are heard in closed session.
- **Use limit.** Register data are used only for draws and checks. No public body may analyse who vouches for whom.

## Where we think it breaks

- **Patient filling of the register.** A school or movement enrols many members whose qualifications are genuine. Nothing false is filed, so no check sees it. If a group holds 20% of a field, it wins a majority of a three-seat panel in about one case in ten. The one-seat-per-source rule, larger panels on disputed cases and the outside seats reduce this, but filtering by opinion is ruled out.
- **Rare fields.** Where only a handful of people in the world practise a specialty, the rest period cannot hold, and the same few people keep being picked.
- **Checks by sampling, not public reading.** Because proofs are sealed, the random re-check catches a single well-made false entry only one time in five; the rest wait for someone to ask for a re-check. The juries' own rulings are also checked only by sample.
- **Workload on citizen juries.** Entry checks, re-checks and audits all fall on citizens picked by lot. We have not measured that load; in a small early network it may be too heavy.
- **Thin paperwork.** A repaired pump or a handwritten statement gives the machine little to read, so trades and poorer regions will be sent to "needs checking" more often. Rates are published by field, region and language, which makes the bias visible, not absent.
- **Small fields.** Where a field has a dozen practitioners in a region, a pseudonym hides no one from the others.

## What we do not know

Nothing on this page has been measured. The capture figures are arithmetic on assumed shares, not observations. The privacy design has had only a first review by the people who wrote it, not by a privacy specialist. No outside critic with experience of real assemblies has read it yet. The outline is accepted in principle but not written into the constitution; both reviews must come first.
