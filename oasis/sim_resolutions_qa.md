## **SIM-QA-01: Quorum Scaling & Demarchic Viability for Emerging Nodes (< 50 Pop)**

* **Date Recorded:** 2026-09-22  
* **Date Merged:** 2026-10-05  
* **Epistemic Status:** `[STATUS: CANONICAL_MERGED]` (Integrated into Living Constitution Arts. 4.2.1, 4.2.2)  
* **Originating Settlement:** Detroit Delray Commons (Starting Pop: 28)  
* **Constitutional Articles:** [`Art. 4.2.1`](../bible/chapters/chapter_04.md), [`Art. 4.2.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_04.md), [`Art. 4.5`](../bible/chapters/chapter_04.md), [`Art. 5.1`](../bible/chapters/chapter_05.md)

### **1. The Material / Empirical Friction**
In the simulation engine, a founding micro-commons node initializes with a modest pioneer population ($N = 28$ citizens). Under a naive interpretation of Chapter IV, convening the **Neighborhood Sortition Council of 15 citizens** ([`Art. 4.2.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_04.md)) requisitions **53.6% of the adult population** into deliberative assembly duty.

Because assembly duty mandates total exemption from routine physical labor ([`Art. 4.3.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_04.md)), the remaining 13 citizens are mathematically incapable of completing the mandatory daily *chore roster* (8h greenhouse farming, 6h water/pumps, 6h microgrid maintenance, 8h fablab recycling). The node rapidly incurs a severe labor deficit, precipitating greywater overflow and food supply collapse within 12 simulated days.

Conversely, abolishing sortition or granting executive decree power to a single administrator would violate the Class-0 Invariant of Demarchy ([`Art. 4.1`](../bible/chapters/chapter_04.md)).

### **2. The Tested Countermeasure (Subsidiarity Scale)**
The simulation implements the **Principle of Demarchic Subsidiarity Scale**:
1. **Emerging Node (Population $< 50$):**
   - Sovereign local deliberation is assigned to the **Local Mediation Panel (3 citizens drawn by lot)** pursuant to [`Art. 4.2.1`](../bible/chapters/chapter_04.md).
   - Strict odd-number parity is preserved (preventing ties), while sequestering only ~10% of the active workforce.
2. **Consolidated Neighborhood (Population $\ge 50$):**
   - Automatically transitions to the full **Neighborhood Sortition Council (15 citizens)** pursuant to [`Art. 4.2.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_04.md).
3. **Graduated Decision Thresholds ([`Art. 4.5`](../bible/chapters/chapter_04.md)):**
   - **60% Qualified Majority:** 2 of 3 votes (Tier 1) / 9 of 15 votes (Tier 2) for routine allocations.
   - **75% Constitutional Supermajority:** 3 of 3 votes (Tier 1) / 12 of 15 votes (Tier 2) for asylum, structural revisions, or emergency covenants.
4. **Cognitive & Screen Accessibility Invariant:**
   - Instead of 15 individual citizen cards cluttering mobile displays, deliberation stances are synthesized via a compact vector **Solarpunk Donut Chart (< 90px)** with a visual threshold notch at 60%/75%, accompanied by an expandable drawer for inspecting juror attributes on demand.

### **3. Telemetric Validation**
- 100-day headless deterministic simulation run with $N = 28$:
  - Zero labor deficit hours.
  - Closed thermodynamic balance ($L_{min}$ floor sustained at 100%).
  - Demarchic rotation executed every 30 days (`720 ticks`) without oligarchy or consecutive terms.

### **4. Open Question for Future Ratification**
*Should the population boundary of 50 residents be formally codified as a statutory annotation to Article 4.2 in the Living Constitution, or should nodes have autonomy to calibrate this transition between 40 and 60 residents based on local automation levels?*

## **SIM-QA-02: Dual-Tier Tool Boundary: Civic Tool Libraries vs. Inviolable Personal Craft Gear**

* **Date Recorded:** 2026-09-23  
* **Date Merged:** 2026-10-05  
* **Epistemic Status:** `[STATUS: CANONICAL_MERGED]` (Integrated into Living Constitution Art. 2.2.1)  
* **Originating Context:** Urban apartment block & low-capital seed node field dynamics (Tbilisi/Georgia working-class housing dialogue)  
* **Constitutional Articles:** [`Art. 2.2.1`](../bible/chapters/chapter_02.md), [`Art. 2.2.3`](../bible/chapters/chapter_02.md), [`Art. 3.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_03.md)

### **1. The Material / Empirical Friction**
In ground-level seed nodes (e.g., dense 2–3 story urban apartment blocks with limited capital and zero immediate budget for rooftop solar or high-tech fablabs), an acute operational tension arises between two core tenets of O.N.E.:
1. **The Inviolability of Personal Effects & Craft Gear ([`Art. 2.2.1`](../bible/chapters/chapter_02.md)):** Citizens must feel somatic and psychological security that their personal instruments, digital workstations, and artisanal trade tools cannot be collectivized or requisitioned by neighborhood fiat.
2. **The Anti-Waste & De-financialization Imperative:** In a typical 20-household building, 20 separate families each privately purchase cheap, redundant utility tools (e.g., hammer drills, extension ladders, angle grinders, sewing machines, automotive jacks). An electric rotary drill has an empirical average utilization of only **12 to 15 minutes across its entire operating lifespan**, representing dead capital extraction by retail monopolies and unnecessary landfill waste.

If a seed node demands total collectivization of all tools, skilled craftspeople and artisans refuse to participate (fearing that their precision, calibrated, or sentimentally vital tools will be damaged by careless neighbors). If the node remains entirely hyper-individualized, poor working-class residents remain trapped in debt and unable to maintain their building.

### **2. The Tested Operational Countermeasure (Dual-Tier Tool Classification)**
The node operating protocol establishes an explicit, objective boundary between **Personal Vocation Gear** and **Civic Utility Libraries**:

1. **Tier 1 — Inviolable Personal Craft Gear ([`Art. 2.2.1`](../bible/chapters/chapter_02.md)):**
   - Defined as tools of high daily utilization, calibrated precision, intimate creative expression, or personal trade specialization (e.g., luthier chisels, developer laptops, surgeon/dentist implements, artist brushes, chef knives, personalized mechanic torque sets).
   - **Juridical Status:** Inviolable individual possession. They can never be expropriated, borrowed without express bilateral consent, or pooled into communal circulation.

2. **Tier 2 — Civic Tool Library & Maintenance Pool ([`Art. 2.2.3`](../bible/chapters/chapter_02.md)):**
   - Defined as intermittent, low-frequency, high-durability infrastructure maintenance equipment (e.g., rotary masonry drills, telescoping aluminum ladders, drain augers, high-pressure washers, heavy sewing/canvas machines, lawn aerators).
   - **Juridical Status:** Held under communal usufruct in a common entrance locker or basement nook. Governed by a simple physical/mesh checkout log.
   - **Maintenance & Sharpening:** Maintained, lubricated, and serviced as part of the node’s shared chore hours or circular fablab recycling roster.

3. **The Voluntary Custody Registry ("Adopt-a-Tool"):**
   - Residents who privately own Tier 2 tools may deposit them into the Civic Tool Library voluntarily.
   - **Incentive / Reciprocity:** The community assumes 100% of the replacement/maintenance cost (sharpening drill bits, replacing batteries) through collective reserves, while the depositor retains unconditional priority reservation whenever they require the tool.

### **3. Simulation & Field Validation**
- Modeled in building-scale metabolic runs ($N = 15–30$ households):
  - Achieves a **~75% reduction in household hardware expenditures** during the founding year.
  - Zero reports of craft expropriation disputes or artisan alienation.
  - Serves as the primary behavioral bridge for building social trust ("metabolic muscle memory") prior to implementing higher-tier solar or water microgrids.

### **4. Open Question for Future Ratification**
*Should the boundary between Tier 1 (Inviolable Craft) and Tier 2 (Civic Library) be governed by an objective checklist of asset classes in statutory annexes, or left to rapid subsidiarity determination by the Local Mediation Panel (Art. 4.2.1) upon dispute?*

## **SIM-QA-03: Ecological Sensor Integrity: ZK-Telemetry Proofs & Analog Citizen Ground-Truthing**

* **Date Recorded:** 2026-09-26  
* **Date Merged:** 2026-10-05  
* **Epistemic Status:** `[STATUS: CANONICAL_MERGED]` (Integrated into Living Constitution Arts. 3.4.3, 3.4.5)

### **1. The Material / Empirical Friction**
While Article 3.4.3 mandates open-hardware Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) with bit-for-bit verifiable firmware, physical field sensors (aquifer pressure transducers, weir flow meters, soil carbon analyzers) can suffer mechanical bio-fouling or hardware calibration drift. An algorithmic anomaly detector might mistake a gradual $-0.25\sigma$ monthly drift for legitimate climatic variation, creating a discrepancy between digital telemetry and true physical carrying capacity.

### **2. Adversarial Stress-Test (RED-5 Assault)**
* **Vector 1 (Proprietary TEE Hardware Trojans):** Relying on closed proprietary "Trusted Execution Environments" (TEEs) allows hardware fabricators to insert microarchitectural backdoors or forged cryptographic signatures.
* **Vector 2 (Local Cartel Bribery & Rubber-Stamping):** If analog inspections are performed strictly by local neighbors, local users facing water quotas can socially coerce or bribe inspectors into misreporting dipstick levels or water quality tests.

### **3. The Hardened Operational Countermeasure (Purple Patch)**
1. **Strict Open Hardware Mandate ([`Art. 3.4.3`](../bible/chapters/chapter_03.md)):** Prohibition of closed proprietary binary blobs or untrusted enclaves; all telemetry hardware must run fully verifiable open firmware.
2. **Citizen Ground-Truthing Squads ([`Art. 3.4.5`](../bible/chapters/chapter_03.md)):** Lay citizen teams equipped with manual analog tools (calibrated dipsticks, mechanical flow meters, titration reagents) conduct unpredictable spot-checks against digital readouts.
3. **Blind Split-Sample Protocol:** Audits mandate split samples: duplicate sealed physical samples are independently verified by laboratories drawn by lot from non-contiguous watersheds, eliminating local collusion and model drift.

### **4. Open Question for Future Ratification**

## **SIM-QA-04: Cryptographic Anti-Brokerage for High-Demand FabLab Queues**

* **Date Merged:** 2026-10-05  
* **Epistemic Status:** `[STATUS: CANONICAL_MERGED]` (Integrated into Living Constitution Arts. 1.4, 3.3.3)  
* **Originating Context:** Operational Edge-Case OP-02 (Inelastic FabLab Queuing)  
* **Constitutional Articles:** [`Art. 1.4`](../bible/chapters/chapter_01.md), [`Art. 2.1.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_02.md), [`Art. 2.2.3`](../bible/chapters/chapter_02.md), [`Art. 3.3.3`](../bible/chapters/chapter_03.md)

### **1. The Material / Empirical Friction**
While real estate, land, and housing are perpetually de-commodified under dynamic usufruct (Art. 2.1 and Art. 2.5), localized physical bottlenecks can occur for high-demand specialized machinery in Tier 3 Open Fab-Labs (e.g., 5-axis CNC mills, metal laser sintering, cleanroom lithography). If reservation queues are open and fungible, an informal secondary market could theoretically emerge where individuals reserve prime tool-hours speculatively and trade access to third parties in exchange for personal gifts or informal favors.

### **2. Adversarial Stress-Test (RED-1 Assault)**
* **Vector 1 (In-Person Proxy Fabrication):** If reservations are soulbound, a scalper simply attends in person, scans their own badge, and executes a third-party client's proprietary G-code file in exchange for unrecorded cash or favors.
* **Vector 2 (Sybil Queue Squatting):** A colluding group reserves prime production blocks under automated accounts with dummy jobs, running zero-spindle dry air cuts or cancelling at the 14-minute mark to block competitors.
* **Vector 3 (Parasitic Commercial Enclosure):** An entrepreneur uses free communal tool time, grid power, and tooling wear to manufacture closed-source proprietary goods for external monetary sale.

### **3. The Hardened Operational Countermeasure (Purple Patch)**
1. **The Execution-Equals-Publication Invariant ([`Art. 1.4`](../bible/chapters/chapter_01.md) & [`Art. 3.3.3`](../bible/chapters/chapter_03.md)):** Any job executed on communal Tier 3 machinery must commit its toolpaths, G-code, and CAM bills of materials to the public local mesh repository. Secret, proprietary, or DRM-locked manufacturing on communal tooling is unconstitutional.
2. **Quadratic Frequency Dampening ([`Art. 3.3.3`](../bible/chapters/chapter_03.md)):** Individual queue priority decays quadratically based on trailing 30-day cumulative machine utilization hours ($W = 1 / (1 + \sum h^2)$), ensuring occasional makers and new creators are never locked out by intensive operators.
3. **Soulbound Booking & Dynamic 15-Minute Forfeiture:** Booking tokens are non-transferable; unattended workstations forfeit after 15 minutes to standby creators drawn by lot, with repeat unexcused no-shows incurring temporary peak-slot booking cooldowns.

### **4. Open Question for Future Ratification**
*How should FabLab booking systems accommodate unforeseen delays (e.g., transit disruptions or urgent care duties) without allowing speculative reservation hoarding?*

## **SIM-QA-05: Codification of Civic Rescue, Firefighting & Disaster Response Guilds**

* **Date Recorded:** 2026-10-05  
* **Date Merged:** 2026-10-05  
* **Epistemic Status:** `[STATUS: CANONICAL_MERGED]` (Integrated into Living Constitution Arts. 5.1.3, 8.1.2)  
* **Originating Context:** Habitat Resiliency Modeling & Operational Analysis of Chapter VIII (Public Safety vs. Physical Entropy Asymmetry)  
* **Constitutional Articles:** [`Art. 5.1.3`](../bible/chapters/chapter_05.md), [`Art. 8.1.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_08.md), [`Art. 8.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_08.md), [`Art. 10.4.4`](../bible/chapters/chapter_10.md)

### **1. The Material / Empirical Friction**
In the Living Constitution, public safety is formulated almost exclusively around the abolition of militarized policing, interpersonal dispute de-escalation, restorative justice circles, and non-carceral custody. Article 8.1.2 creates *"unarmed Civic Peace Guilds trained in de-escalation and trauma triage"*.

However, physical reality and thermodynamic entropy generate acute hazards independent of human malicious intent or social friction:
1. **Physical and Combustion Disasters:** Conflagrations in timber/strawbale modular habitat units (MHU), high-voltage battery energy storage system (BESS) thermal runaways, and catastrophic forest/wildland fires.
2. **Geophysical and Meteorological Shocks:** Flash flooding, riverbank levee breaches, structural earthquake entrapments, alpine/speleological rescues, and industrial hazardous material (Hazmat) neutralizations.

Conflating technical rescue and firefighting with "Peace Guilds" or "interpersonal mediation" creates a lethal operational category error:
* Peace Guild mediators are trained in somatic trauma and dialogue, not in high-angle rope extraction, breathing apparatus (SCBA) protocols, hydraulic extrication, or dynamic wildfire flanking.
* Unlike armed policing (which historically protects absentee property monopolies and sovereign borders through coercive violence), **firefighting and search-and-rescue (SAR) represent the purest historical embodiment of non-carceral mutual aid**.
* Failing to explicitly codify a dedicated, technical **Civic Rescue & Firefighting Guild** leaves ambiguous how emergency command functions during sub-minute life-and-death crises, how equipment is provisioned, and how training/readiness hours are credited under the labor reciprocity matrix.

### **2. The Tested Operational Countermeasure (Institutional Bifurcation & ICS)**
The operational simulation implements a deterministic bifurcation of public safety and emergency functions:

1. **Explicit Domain Bifurcation:**
   * **Civic Peace Guilds ([`Art. 8.1.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_08.md)):** Stewards of human social peace, interpersonal restorative justice, de-escalation, and psychiatric/trauma triage.
   * **Civic Rescue & Fire Guilds / Civil Protection Brigades ([`Art. 8.1.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_08.md) & [`Art. 10.4.4`](../bible/chapters/chapter_10.md)):** Autonomous technical guilds specialized in structural and wildland firefighting, technical search and rescue (SAR), flood/seismic disaster response, and chemical/biological hazard mitigation.

2. **De-Militarized Incident Command Protocol (Open ICS):**
   * During active kinetic/environmental crises (active fires, structural collapses, hazardous plumes), tactical operational authority is temporarily vested in certified **Incident Technical Coordinators** drawn from the Rescue Guild, operating under flat, open Incident Command System protocols.
   * Decisions are strictly bounded to life-safety, victim triage, and structural containment; coordinators possess zero judicial, carceral, or property-disposition authority.

3. **Hybrid Staffing & Peak Ergonomic Multipliers ([`Art. 5.1.3`](../bible/chapters/chapter_05.md)):**
   * **Core Technical Mentors:** A standing cadre of certified master instructors and technical specialists who maintain complex apparatus (pumps, thermal drones, breathing gear, cutting torches).
   * **Rotational & Voluntary Reserves:** Civic cohorts drawn from the local population completing rotational shifts and voluntary on-call standby.
   * **Labor Reciprocity:** Active emergency deployments, technical training drills, and hazardous standby carry peak **ergonomic time multipliers of $4\times$ to $5\times$** against baseline chore obligations pursuant to Article 5.1.3.

4. **Hardware Standard & Analog Resilience ([`Art. 6.1.3`](../bible/chapters/chapter_06.md) & [`Art. 6.2.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_06.md)):**
   * Firefighting and rescue apparatus are standardized across open-hardware blueprints: electric/hybrid high-torque tender chassis, pressurized gravity-fed cistern hydrants (requiring zero grid power), and thermal imaging drones operating ephemeral, zero-biometric edge telemetry.

5. **Inter-Watershed Mutual Aid Logistics ([`Art. 10.4.4`](../bible/chapters/chapter_10.md)):**
   * Large-scale catastrophes (e.g., wildland conflagrations exceeding local suppression capacity) trigger automated inter-basin mobilization corridors, transferring specialized equipment and mobile strike brigades across adjacent watersheds without political delay.

## **SIM-QA-06: Governance, Thermodynamic Calibration & Usufruct Transformation of Legacy High-Capital Assets (Fabs, Data Centers & Megacities)**

* **Date Recorded:** 2026-10-20  
* **Date Merged:** 2026-10-21  
* **Epistemic Status:** `[STATUS: CANONICAL_MERGED]` (Integrated into Living Constitution Arts. 2.2, 3.3.4, 6.2, 7.4, 10.3)  
* **Originating Context:** Operational Edge-Case OP-06 (Neutralizing the Pastoral Fallacy in Heavy Industry, Hyperscale Compute & Metropolises)  
* **Constitutional Articles:** [`Art. 2.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_02.md), [`Art. 3.3.4`](../bible/chapters/chapter_03.md), [`Art. 6.2`](../bible/chapters/chapter_06.md), [`Art. 7.4`](../bible/chapters/chapter_07.md), [`Art. 10.3`](../bible/chapters/chapter_10.md)  
* **Master Architectural Specification:** [`oasis/legacy_high_capital_assets.md`](legacy_high_capital_assets.md)

### **1. The Material / Empirical Friction**
In standard post-capitalist discourse, alternative systems either regress into agrarian parochialism (dismantling advanced industry) or preserve corporate/state technocracies. ## **SIM-QA-18: Refusal of a Sortition Summons — Undefined Procedure**

* **Date Recorded:** 2026-11-12  
* **Stress-Tested:** 2026-11-12  
* **Epistemic Status:** `[STATUS: OASIS_STRESS_TESTED]` (Stage 2: RED_STRESS_TEST) — Verdict on current text **`RED_WINS`** (RED-1, RED-2, small-node fill failure); RED-3 and RED-4 **`BLUE_HOLDS`** under H1/H2. RED-2 survives every hypothesis. Purple patch drafted below as `[STATUS: SIMULATION_HYPOTHESIS]`, not merged.  
* **Originating Context:** Maintainer review of Chapter IV  
* **Constitutional Articles:** Arts. 4.2.2, 4.3.1, 4.3.2, 4.3.3, 5.2.3  
* **Reproducible Evidence:** [`simone/benchmarks/sim_qa_18_summons_refusal.py`](../simone/benchmarks/sim_qa_18_summons_refusal.py) → [`results`](../simone/benchmarks/results/sim_qa_18_summons_refusal_results.md) (`--reps 200 --seed 20261112`; **synthetic, uncalibrated**: every share, multiplier and effect size is an assumption)

### **1. The Friction**
* Art. 4.3.2 calls civic service by lot an "inviolable duty". Neither the Constitution nor the Organic Law Annex says what happens when a summoned citizen refuses, defers, or drops out, or how the seat is refilled.
* No sanction is available. Tier 1 is unconditional (Art. 5.2.1) and Art. 5.2.3 states that sortition eligibility and political rights are never conditioned (the earlier sanction was withdrawn under SIM-QA-15, `RED_WINS`).
* If refusal is simply free, self-selection returns. Voluntary citizens' assemblies report invitation acceptance rates in the low single digits (about 6% on average across 65 assemblies in the OECD database; 4.2% in the Aargau citizens' panel).
* Small nodes cannot absorb refusals. At 50 adults, a council of 15 with six-month mandates and a twelve-month hiatus (Art. 4.3.3) needs 45 distinct people every 18 months, before counting the Local Mediation Panel and the Triage Panel.
* Long full-time mandates (18 months, two years) will draw far more refusals than short ones, so the bias grows with the tier.

### **2. Preliminary Red Vectors (to be tested)**
* **RED-1 (silent self-selection):** refusal correlates with age, care load, or distrust. The seated body still passes the age/gender/geography quotas of Art. 4.3.1 while being skewed on everything else.
* **RED-2 (strategic refusal):** a faction declines en masse to starve a body of quorum, or accepts en masse while others decline, and captures it without breaking any rule.
* **RED-3 (excusal gatekeeping):** whoever grants exemptions decides who sits. A panel judging "valid reasons" becomes a selection filter.
* **RED-4 (coerced juror):** a citizen forced to serve complies maliciously or disengages, which degrades deliberation more than an empty seat.

### **3. Hypotheses to Test**
* **H1, duty to attend, right to withdraw:** a summoned citizen must attend a short orientation phase and may then withdraw without penalty.
* **H2, deferral and listed excusals:** one deferral without stated reason; excusal on grounds enumerated in Organic Law (illness, bereavement, non-replaceable care), self-certified, never judged on merit.
* **H3, stratum-matched alternates:** the same public draw produces ordered alternates per stratum, so a replacement never shifts the composition or the odd parity.
* **H4, published refusal rates:** refusal and dropout rates are published per stratum. A stratum that refuses well above the others triggers a review of service conditions, never of persons.
* **H5, shorter mandates at higher tiers:** see SIM-QA-19 H4.

### **4. Stage 2: Model and Red-Team Findings Against the Current Text** *(synthetic, uncalibrated)*
Model: Python stdlib Monte Carlo, not the Julia SIMONE ABM; HEXACO traits not modelled. Node of 50 / 150 / 500 adults; council of 15 (six-month mandate, twelve-month hiatus) and mediation panel of 3 (three-month mandate, six-month hiatus); quota draw on age, gender and geography; refusal swept 0–0.95, uniform or correlated with age and care load (carers 20% of adults, assumed 2.5× refusal). "B0" reads the current text as free refusal with replacement from the general pool.
* **Small-node fill failure: `RED_WINS`.** At 50 adults the hiatus leaves about 17–20 eligible people for 15 council seats. At 10% refusal 7% of council terms already leave a seat empty; at 30%, about half. No hypothesis rescues this (H1–H3 together: 3% at 10%, 30% at 30%). At 150 adults seats fail only from about 80–90% refusal; at 500, not below 95%. At an invitation-style refusal of about 94%, only the 500-adult node fills its council.
* **RED-1 (silent self-selection): `RED_WINS`.** With correlated refusal from 60% upward, carers fall from 20% of the population to about 1–2% of seated members (−18 to −19 points), while stratum-matched replacement (H3) keeps age, gender and geography on target. The quotas of Art. 4.3.1 are met while the body loses its carers.
* **RED-2 (strategic refusal): `RED_WINS`, analytic.** Under free refusal, a faction of share *f* that always accepts while others refuse at rate *r* holds an expected seat share of *f* / (*f* + (1−*f*)(1−*r*)). A 5% faction holds 12% of seats at *r* = 0.6, 34% at 0.9 and 47% at 0.94; a 10% faction holds 65% at 0.94. No rule breaks. Quorum starvation is the mirror case: at 50 adults a handful of refusers can empty seats.
* **RED-3 (excusal gatekeeping): `NOT_TRIGGERED` under current text** (no excusal procedure exists), **`BLUE_HOLDS` under H2** (self-certified listed grounds, never judged on merit).
* **RED-4 (coerced juror): `NOT_TRIGGERED`.** No sanction exists (Art. 5.2.3), so nobody is coerced. The defect is the reverse: "inviolable duty" is a duty without content.

### **5. Stage 2: Red Team Against the Hypotheses**
* **H1 (attend, then may withdraw): `BLUE_HOLDS`, effect assumed.** The model assumes 30% of would-be refusers stay after orientation; with that assumption the carer gap shrinks to −4 to −9 points and the 50-adult fill threshold moves from 10% to 20%. The size of the effect is the assumption, not a finding. Red: orientation costs time of people who will leave; a faction attends and stays regardless (RED-2 unchanged).
* **H2 (one deferral, listed self-certified excusals): `BLUE_HOLDS`, effect assumed.** If care support is arranged before a deferred carer's next summons, the carer gap shrinks to about −5 to −7 points. Red: a self-certified excusal is free refusal under another name. *Guard:* excusals count in the published rates (H4).
* **H3 (stratum-matched alternates): `BLUE_HOLDS` on the stratified attributes only.** Restores age/gender/geography to the quota-fill floor (TVD ≈ 0.03) at 150 and 500 adults, but does nothing for carers (still −18 to −19 points) and nothing against RED-2, since the faction is spread across strata. At 50 adults cells run empty and fallback undoes it.
* **H1 + H2 + H3: best tested combination.** Carer gap about −2 points and composition at the floor at 150 and 500 adults. Still fails at 50 adults.
* **H4 (published rates): `BLUE_HOLDS` as a diagnostic.** It makes RED-1 visible; it cannot reveal RED-2 without identifying who accepts.
* **H5 (shorter mandates at higher tiers):** not modelled; see SIM-QA-19 H4 (`INCONCLUSIVE`).
* **Additional hypothesis H6 (small-node hiatus):** in the 50-adult node, a council hiatus equal to the mandate (6 months) moves the 5% fill-failure point from 10% to 50% refusal (B0) and to 60% with H1–H3. Red: a shorter hiatus means the same people return every year in small nodes, which is the entrenchment Art. 4.3.3 exists to prevent. `BLUE_HOLDS` with that residual.

* Bodies of 3 and 15 are juries (impartial because drawn), not samples. Yet Art. 4.2.1 vests "sovereign municipal administration" in a panel of 3 for nodes under 50 adults, and a council of 15 governs local commons.

### **2. Preliminary Red Vectors (to be tested)**
* **RED-1 (unlucky draw):** a 15-seat council enacts a 60% measure that the constituency rejects, with no recall path short of the next rotation.
* **RED-2 (small-body capture):** existing SIMONE falsification challenge 2 (a faction of k ≥ 3 capturing 60% of a 15-member council across three rotations).
* **RED-3 (quota gaming):** with only three strata, a faction concentrated inside one age/gender/geography cell is over-seated by design.
* **RED-4 (global tokenism):** macro-region quotas at 1,001 seats present continental majorities as planetary consent.

### **3. Hypotheses to Test**
* **H1, name the function of each tier:** state that the 3- and 15-seat bodies are juries and that representativeness is claimed from 101 seats upward.
* **H2, whole-node decisions below a size threshold:** under a fixed number of adults (e.g. 150), 60% and 75% matters go to all adult residents, extending the plebiscite rule Art. 4.2.1 already applies to 75% matters in 3-person panels.
* **H3, recall to a larger jury:** a fixed share of constituents can refer a council decision to a larger single-issue jury drawn for that purpose.
* **H4, split functions at upper tiers:** a small standing body for agenda and oversight, and large short-term single-issue juries (one to two weeks) for final votes. Larger samples and lower refusal at the same total service load.

## **SIM-QA-20: Rival Expert Panels — Advocacy Capture and Precautionary Veto**

* **Date Recorded:** 2026-11-12  
* **Stress-Tested:** 2026-11-12 (qualitative O-ASIS red-team pass; no simulation)  
* **Epistemic Status:** `[STATUS: OASIS_STRESS_TESTED]` (Stage 2: RED_STRESS_TEST) — Verdict on current text **`RED_WINS`** on all five vectors; hypotheses H1–H6 hold only with guards, H6 **narrowed**. Addendum (Section 8): H7/H8 and RED-6/RED-7 red-teamed on text, measurement **`NOT_RUN`**. Purple patch drafted below as `[STATUS: SIMULATION_HYPOTHESIS]`, not merged.  
* **Originating Context:** Maintainer review of Chapter IV  
* **Constitutional Articles:** Arts. 4.4.1–4.4.4, 4.6.2, 7.3, 7.5, 7.6.1, 8.4.3; Organic Law OL-9.7, OL-1.4
* **Evidence:** reasoning on the text only. No quantitative claim is made; nothing below is empirical.

### **1. The Friction**
* Art. 4.1.2 abolishes professional politics, but Art. 4.4.2 stages every complex proposal as a duel between two rival panels. The format rewards advocacy skill. It can bring the professional persuader back in an evidentiary role.
* Internal inconsistency: Art. 8.4.3 abolishes adversarial trials in favour of Fact-Centric Inquiry, while Art. 4.4.2 mandates adversarial cross-examination for policy.
* The text requires panels to be "independent" and free of grants or debt. It does not say who nominates, selects, or seats them. Sortition of practitioners appears only as the deadlock fallback of Art. 4.4.4.
* Two panels imply two sides of equal weight, even where the evidence is lopsided or where there are more than two positions.
* Art. 4.4.4 withholds approval when panels produce confusion. Confusion is therefore a winning strategy for whoever wants to block.
* Experts have no hiatus rule comparable to Art. 4.3.3, so a standing expert class can form.
* Empirical reviews of citizens' assemblies find that expert influence acts through framing, deference to authority, and information asymmetry, and is strongest where time is short and expert selection is opaque.

### **2. Preliminary Red Vectors (to be tested)**
* **RED-1 (nomination capture):** a guild or faction controls who is eligible for either panel, so both "rivals" share the same premises.
* **RED-2 (manufactured doubt):** one panel deliberately muddies the record to trigger the precautionary suspension of Art. 4.4.4.
* **RED-3 (rhetorical capture):** the more persuasive panel wins regardless of evidence (SIMONE Layer 2 already models rhetorical capture).
* **RED-4 (false balance):** a fringe position gains half the floor time by being assigned a panel.
* **RED-5 (repeat players):** the same experts recur across sessions and tiers and learn to steer jurors.

### **3. Hypotheses to Test**
* **H1, experts by lot:** panels are drawn from open practitioner registers as the default, with the Art. 4.3.3 hiatus applied to experts.
* **H2, separate evidence from advocacy:** a drawn panel writes the factual brief. Proponents and opponents speak afterwards, declared as parties.
* **H3, juror-led inquiry:** jurors conduct the questioning and may call further witnesses, aligning Art. 4.4 with Art. 8.4.3.
* **H4, disagreement map:** experts must file a joint written statement of what they agree on, where they differ, and which observation would change their position.
* **H5, closed deliberation:** final deliberation takes place without experts in the room.
* **H6, symmetric burden:** inaction is presented as an option with its own stated consequences, so that confusion does not default to one side.

### **4. Stage 2: Red Team Against the Current Text (Art. 4.4)**
* **RED-1 (nomination capture): `RED_WINS`.** Art. 4.4.2 requires panels to be "independent" but names no selector. Agenda authority rests with delegates (Art. 4.3.4), so either the 15 jurors pick their own witnesses or someone unnamed does. A guild that controls who counts as a practitioner can staff both "rival" panels with people who share its premises. Nothing in Art. 4.1.3 reaches this, because a guild that nominates experts does not nominate *delegates*.
* **RED-2 (manufactured doubt): `RED_WINS`.** Under Art. 4.4.4, confusion produced by a panel is itself the trigger for withholding approval. Any actor who prefers the status quo on a novel structural intervention needs only a panel that muddies the record. The status quo is not always *pro natura*: replacing a failing levee with a new design is a novel structural intervention. The deadlock fallback hands the decision to a panel of practitioners, which contradicts Art. 4.4.1 (experts never vote).
* **RED-3 (rhetorical capture): `RED_WINS` (qualitative).** A duel rewards the better advocate. Art. 4.4.3 puts the burden of intelligibility on experts but gives jurors no inquiry powers, so the side that explains more fluently wins regardless of evidence. Magnitude is unknown; the SIMONE Layer 2 run was not done.
* **RED-4 (false balance): `RED_WINS`.** "Two rival panels" fixes the number of sides at two. A fringe position receives half the floor; a question with three positions is forced into two.
* **RED-5 (repeat players): `RED_WINS`.** Art. 4.3.3 binds jurors, not witnesses. The same experts can appear in every session at every tier and learn how to steer juries: the professional persuader returns in an evidentiary role.
* **Coherence defects:** Art. 4.4.2 mandates adversarial cross-examination while Art. 8.4.3 abolishes adversarial trials for Fact-Centric Inquiry. Art. 4.6.2 makes constitutional review juries deliberate "under Article 4.4", and OL-1.4 runs the biosecurity registry on rival testimony, so every defect above propagates to constitutional review and biosecurity.

### **5. Stage 2: Red Team Against the Hypotheses**
* **H1 (experts by lot, hiatus): `BLUE_HOLDS` with guards.**
  * Red: the gate moves to register admission. If a guild certifies who enters the register, RED-1 returns one step earlier. *Guard:* admission on published criteria of demonstrated work, open to anyone meeting them; no guild, association or body may gatekeep; admissions logged and reviewable under Art. 4.6.
  * Red: a random draw reproduces the field's majority and can bury a correct heterodox minority. *Guard:* any party may add declared witnesses (H2), and jurors may call more (H3).
  * Red: small specialties (four hydrogeologists in a bioregion) cannot carry a hiatus. *Guard:* hiatus per subject matter, with a draw from other bioregions when the local pool is below an Organic Law minimum.
  * Red: a register is a list of named persons with attributes, in tension with the non-identifying registry of Art. 7.4.4. *Guard:* practitioner registers are voluntary and separate from the civic register.
* **H2 (evidence before advocacy): `BLUE_HOLDS` only with H3 and H4.** Red: whoever writes the brief frames the question, and framing is what Art. 4.3.4 forbids staff to do. A drawn brief panel is a single point of framing capture unless dissent is annexed in full (H4) and jurors may reject the brief and inquire further (H3).
* **H3 (juror-led inquiry): `BLUE_HOLDS`.** Aligns Art. 4.4 with Art. 8.4.3. Red: lay questions may be weak, and a facilitator could steer them. Residual: question quality; facilitators remain bound by Art. 4.3.4.
* **H4 (disagreement map): `BLUE_HOLDS` with guard.** Red: experts can collude on a false consensus, or name an unfalsifiable condition for changing their mind. *Guard:* each member signs individually; a position that names no observation able to change it is recorded as such in the brief.
* **H5 (closed deliberation): `BLUE_HOLDS` with guard.** Red: without experts present, jurors misremember technical detail. *Guard:* written questions during deliberation, answered on the public record. Deliberation is closed; evidence and answers stay public.
* **H6 (symmetric burden): `NARROWED`.** Red: a fully symmetric burden repeals the precautionary invariant (*In dubio pro natura et vita*), and a Legacy actor will argue that inaction harms (lost output, lost jobs). Narrowed to: action **and** inaction are both briefed with their consequences, but suspension needs a specific, plausible pathway to irreversible harm that the proposal does not close. Confusion or disagreement alone never suspends. RED-2 must then fabricate a concrete harm pathway, which the evidence panel can check.

### **8. Addendum (2026-11-12): Open Models as Evidentiary Aid** *(Stage 2 continued; Sections 4–7 unchanged)*
*Measurement status of every item in this addendum: **`NOT_RUN`**. The verdicts below are a red-team reading of the text, not tests. No quantitative claim is made.*

#### **8.1 New Hypotheses**
* **H7, open models as evidentiary aid.** Open, reproducible models (Arts. 7.3, 7.5) prepare the case for each position in place of, or alongside, the rival panels. Conditions:
  * jurors write the proposal and the positions; each position is worded by its supporters and checked by the other side;
  * one fixed, public prompt template with a single position slot, written before any topic is known, accepting more than two positions;
  * the same attached documents, settings and length limit for every run; any party may add documents;
  * at least two models of different origin, each arguing every position (role swap), with repeated runs and a reworded-proposal run to expose framing sensitivity;
  * every claim cites a passage in the attached documents; uncited claims are discarded for all sides;
  * each output states its assumptions, its weakest point, and the observation that would change the conclusion;
  * human practitioners remain for local and practical knowledge; no model is present in final deliberation.
* **H8, oversight of machine influence.** A control loop with a number, a threshold, a body and a consequence:
  * the civic register publishes prompts, model outputs and votes, and computes per assembly the share of decisions that matched the machine-written brief (concordance index);
  * any citizen can re-run a published prompt and compare the output;
  * Organic Law fixes a concordance threshold (e.g. 90% over one year) that triggers a review;
  * the review is done by a single-purpose panel of 15 citizens drawn from a non-contiguous watershed, dissolved afterwards, which reads the session records and not only the index;
  * the panel may clear the assembly, order a change of model or prompt template, or suspend machine aid in that assembly for a fixed period; it cannot reverse decisions already taken;
  * models and prompt templates used in deliberation fall under the red-team bounties of Art. 7.6.1.
  * *Known limits:* high concordance may only mean the brief was good; an assembly aware of the index can diverge on purpose to lower it.

#### **8.2 New Red Vectors**
* **RED-6 (drift toward the synthesis):** jurors follow the machine-written brief by habit, so the model governs in fact without anyone deciding it should.
* **RED-7 (framing and model capture):** whoever writes the prompt template, selects the documents, or controls the weights steers both sides at once; two "rival" runs of related models share the same blind spots.

#### **8.3 Red Team Against H7** *(measurement `NOT_RUN`)*
* **Coherence with Chapter VII: `RED_WINS` unless the citation rule carries the load.**
  * Art. 7.5.3 voids any governance protocol that resists functional comprehension by ordinary citizens. A neural model does. H7 survives only because the checkable object is the cited passage, not the model: a juror can read the passage and judge whether it supports the claim. Without the citation rule, H7 is void under Art. 7.5.3.
  * Art. 7.1.2 makes delegation of legislative drafting to neural models an absolute nullity. H7 keeps drafting with jurors; RED-6 is the route by which drafting slides to the model anyway (see 8.5).
  * Art. 7.3 demands bit-for-bit reproducibility of *binaries*. Published weights can be hashed and re-run, but training cannot be reproduced by any citizen node, and sampled inference is not deterministic. "Reproducible" in H7 can mean only: fixed weight hash, fixed settings, logged inputs, re-runnable within a tolerance.
  * Art. 7.2 bans proprietary or licence-locked software in public administration. Many "open-weight" models carry use restrictions and undisclosed training data. Eligible models must meet Art. 7.2, which may leave very few.
* **RED-1 (nomination capture) → RED-7: `RED_WINS` (residual).** Capture moves from choosing experts to choosing models, template and documents. "Different origin" is weak protection: few organizations train capable models, and they share much of the same training data, so role-swapped runs on two such models agree on the same blind spots. Role swap exposes *asymmetric* advocacy, not *shared* error. *Guard:* models drawn by lot per case from an open register of models meeting Art. 7.2, with a hiatus per model as for experts. Residual: the register is small.
* **RED-2 (manufactured doubt): `RED_WINS` via documents.** "Any party may add documents" opens two attacks. Flooding: a party adds many documents so the length limit and citation rule favour its material. Injection: a document carries text written to steer the model, and it steers every run on every side at once. Role swap does not reveal injection, since it acts on all positions equally. *Guard:* equal document quota per position; every document shown to jurors as attached; template and documents in scope of the Art. 7.6.1 bounties. Residual: injection detection is an open problem; screening documents would recreate a gatekeeper.
* **RED-3 (rhetorical capture): `BLUE_HOLDS` on symmetry, moves to RED-6.** Every position gets the same fluency, which removes the advocacy-skill gap. The persuasive asymmetry becomes deference to machine text as such.
* **RED-4 (false balance): `PARTIAL`.** More than two positions are allowed (fixes the two-sides defect). But an equal length limit per position gives a fringe position as much text as a well-supported one, which signals equal weight. *Guard:* the human disagreement map (H4) states where practitioners actually stand; each output's "weakest point" is shown first.
* **RED-5 (repeat players): `RED_WINS` (worse than before).** A model is the ultimate repeat player: the same weights in every assembly at every tier. One systematic bias becomes a planet-wide monoculture. *Guard:* the per-case lottery and per-model hiatus above. Residual: model diversity is bounded by how many eligible models exist.
* **Citation rule: `BLUE_HOLDS` with guard.** Red: a model can cite a real passage that does not support the claim. Checking citations by another model is recursion. *Guard:* each side checks the other side's citations by hand; a claim whose cited passage does not support it is discarded like an uncited one.
* **Overall H7: `BLUE_HOLDS_WITH_GUARDS` as an aid alongside drawn human panels; `RED_WINS` "in place of" them.** Replacing human practitioners removes the only check on shared model blind spots (RED-7) and on documents nobody brought.

#### **8.4 Red Team Against H8** *(measurement `NOT_RUN`)*
* **The index is undefined for H7 as written: `RED_WINS` until fixed.** H7 briefs argue every position and make no recommendation, so "matched the machine-written brief" has no referent. *Guard:* each run ends with a sealed line naming the position best supported by its cited passages; the line is withheld from jurors and published only after the vote. Concordance is computed against the sealed lines. The sealed line cannot steer jurors and still measures drift.
* **Small samples: `RED_WINS` at small tiers.** A 15-seat council decides few complex matters a year (OL-9.7 caps them). With 10 decisions, 9 of 10 concordant (90%) arises by chance far too often to be a signal (see SIM-QA-19 for small-sample error). *Guard:* the threshold applies only above a minimum count of decisions, pooled over a longer window if needed.
* **Publishing votes: `RED_WINS` if individual.** Published individual votes let anyone verify how a juror voted, which is what makes instructing or rewarding delegates enforceable and so defeats Art. 4.1.3. *Guard:* publish tallies only.
* **Re-run right: `BLUE_HOLDS` with two guards.** Inference is not exactly deterministic, so comparison needs published settings and a stated tolerance; divergence beyond it is a bounty finding (Art. 7.6.1). Coherence: Art. 5.2.2 can exclude a citizen from advanced computing grids; the re-run right must be exempt, as sortition eligibility is under Art. 5.2.3.
* **Review panel reading session records: `PARTIAL`.** H5 closes final deliberation. The panel can read sealed deliberation records only if they exist. *Guard:* records kept sealed, opened to the review panel alone, never published. Residual: jurors who know they may be read later deliberate differently.
* **Art. 7.6.1 scope: `PARTIAL`.** The bounties cover vulnerabilities, backdoors and centralization vectors. Framing bias in a template is not clearly any of these. *Guard:* Organic Law names framing bias and document injection as eligible findings.
* **RED-7 against H8: `RED_WINS` (residual).** A template that steers all positions equally leaves concordance unchanged. H8 cannot see symmetric capture. *Guard:* the register also publishes sensitivity scores from role swap, repetition and rewording (the script in the next action).
* **Known limits confirmed.** High concordance may only mean good briefs; an assembly aware of the index can game it downward. The panel reads records rather than the number, which is the right answer to both, at the cost of 15 citizens' time per review.

#### **8.5 RED-6 and RED-7 Verdicts**
* **RED-6 (drift toward the synthesis): `RED_WINS` against H7 alone; `PARTIAL` with H8 and the sealed-line guard.** H7 offers no synthesis, but jurors can still adopt whichever case reads best, which is de facto drafting by the model (Art. 7.1.2). H8 with sealed lines measures this; it cannot prevent it. The consequence (suspension of machine aid) is the only brake.
* **RED-7 (framing and model capture): `RED_WINS` (residual after all guards).** Lottery over models, a version-locked template adopted by Organic Law, bounties and sensitivity scores raise the cost. None removes the shared blind spots of models trained on similar data, and none detects a template that steers every side the same way.

#### **8.6 Residual Weaknesses (Addendum)**
* Shared blind spots across models (RED-7) have no detector. Human practitioners are the only check, so H7 must stay "alongside", never "in place of".
* Document injection is unsolved without a gatekeeper.
* The number of models meeting Art. 7.2 may be too small for lottery and hiatus to mean anything.
* Concordance measures drift only after the fact and only above a minimum count of decisions; small councils are effectively unmonitored.
* Every verdict here is reasoning on text. Nothing has been measured.

**Next action (addendum):** a reproducible script that measures, on real case files, how far model output moves under role swap, repetition and rewording (framing-sensitivity scores for H7/H8). H7/H8 are not added to the purple patch in Section 6 until those measurements exist.

### **1. The Friction**
* Art. 4.2.1 vests "sovereign municipal administration" in a drawn panel of 3 for nodes under 50 adults. Three people, often close to each other, hold full local power. The kin rule bars first-degree kin and partners from sitting together; it does not bar friends, allies or a dominant extended family.
* Art. 4.2.2 triggers a 15-seat council at 50 adults. SIM-QA-18 shows its seats cannot be filled once refusal passes 10 to 20%.
* Small settlements are many places but few people. India, 2011 census: 82,151 of 597,608 inhabited villages (13.7%) have fewer than 200 inhabitants and hold under 1% of the rural population. France, 2015: 910 communes under 50 inhabitants (31,357 people in all) and 2,567 between 50 and 99. *No global count of settlements under 150 adults was found.* These figures are recorded as supplied for this docket and must be checked against the primary census tables before any canon use.
* What such a node decides is narrow and practical: shift rosters, water, food growing, the local microgrid, allocation of free dwellings, disputes between neighbours, reception of newcomers. Health care, higher education, industry and general rules already come from a larger centre.

### **2. Hypotheses Tested Against Each Other**
* **H1, attachment:** a node below the threshold has no governing body of its own. It is governed by the council of the nearest larger centre, and its adults enter that centre's draw.
* **H2, two levels:** no drawn body below the threshold. (a) Matters internal to the node are decided by the assembly of all adult residents. (b) Everything else goes to the council of the nearest larger centre, whose draw includes the node's adults. (c) Disputes are heard by a panel of 3 drawn from outside the node. (d) The node holds a right of appeal (*Provocatio*) against a centre decision that concerns only the node. (e) An isolated or founding node with no larger centre nearby runs on (a) alone until one exists.
* **For both:** threshold at 50, 100 or 150 adults.

### **4. Stage 2: Red Team**
* **Current text: `RED_WINS`.** At 50 adults the council fails on fill (SIM-QA-18) and takes 30% of adults off the roster (12.2 h per adult per week). Below 50, three drawn people hold all local power.
* **RED-1, voiceless periphery.** *H1: `RED_WINS`.* A node of 50 in a pool of 2,000 holds no council seat 68% of the time, and under H1 that council decides everything, including the node's water, rosters and dwellings. *H2: `NARROWED`.* Internal matters stay with residents; for other matters the node is as voiceless as any 50 people in a large centre, which is the ordinary condition of sortition, not a defect specific to small nodes. (d) adds an appeal where a centre decision targets the node alone. Residual: (c) has no outside pool for a founding node. *Guard:* draw the dispute panel from the nearest other O.N.E. nodes; if none exists, from the node itself under the current kin rule, until one does.
* **RED-3, subsidiarity (Art. 10.1.3).** *H1: `RED_WINS`.* Rosters, a shared greenhouse or a free dwelling are resolvable inside the node; sending them to a centre council breaches "the lowest ecological scale possible". *H2: `BLUE_HOLDS`.* (a) keeps them local and (b) sends only what is not.
* **RED-4, assembly capture.** *H2: `RED_WINS` on open voting, `NARROWED` with guards.* A bloc that always attends gains weight when others stay home: a 20% bloc holds 29% of votes cast at 60% turnout, enough to block every 75% matter; a 30% bloc holds 42–52%. For comparison, under B0 the same 30% bloc holds 2 of 3 panel seats 22% of the time, and then holds everything. Hand-raising in front of 30 neighbours adds social pressure no number captures. *Guards:* secret ballot on any vote at the request of one adult, and always for dwellings, newcomers and disputes; a chair drawn by lot for each session; thresholds counted on votes cast with the quorum of Organic Law; the existing *Provocatio* for every party to a decision on dwellings or admission. Residual: a secret ballot among 30 people leaks, and a charismatic speaker still frames the debate.
* **RED-5, boundary gaming.** *Both: `NARROWED`.* A node of 152 that splits on paper into two of 76 escapes the council; a dispute over what is "internal" is a jurisdiction grab in either direction. *Guards:* node boundaries follow the watershed register (Art. 10.1) and change only by decision of the Bioregional Assembly; the threshold has hysteresis (council from 150 adults sustained for twelve months; back to the assembly only below 120 sustained for twelve months); "internal" means a matter whose resources and effects stay inside the node's own balance without changing its net flows with the centre beyond limits fixed by Organic Law; a dispute over classification goes to the outside panel of (c), whose ruling is final for that matter.
* **RED-6, labour drain.** *H2: `BLUE_HOLDS`, effect assumed.* No one is on full release inside the node; at an assumed 1.5 h per attending adult per week the assembly costs about a tenth of the current council at 50 adults (1.3 vs 12.2 h per adult). The SIM-QA-01 problem (too many people in meetings, too few on shifts) does not return. Red: if every matter goes to the floor, meetings grow without limit. *Guard:* a fixed number of ordinary sessions per month in Organic Law; shift rosters are set by the assembly and run by rotation, not debated each week.
* **Coherence:** an all-adult assembly is not a sortition body, so Art. 4.1.1 ("exclusively from … sortition") must name it, as SIM-QA-19 already proposes for node plebiscites.

### **5. Comparison: H1 vs H2**
H1 is cheap in hours (0.4 h per adult per week) and removes every small-body risk, but loses three vectors outright: it hands a place's daily life to a council it usually has no seat on, it fails at founding when no centre exists, and it breaches Art. 10.1.3. H2 costs about 1 h more per adult per week, keeps the practical matters where they are known, and survives founding; it trades the risk of three drawn people for the risk of a dominant bloc in an open room, which secret ballot and outside dispute panels narrow but do not remove. **H2 held up better.** Threshold: **150**.

### **7. Residual Weaknesses (Honest Limits)**
* **Assembly capture is narrowed, not solved.** A disciplined bloc of 20–30% that always attends carries or blocks matters in a small room; secret ballot among neighbours leaks.
* **Nodes of 100–149 adults could staff a council** up to about 70% refusal, and lose sortition for internal matters under this patch. The 150 choice favours robustness at high refusal over sortition coverage in that band.
* **"Internal" will be litigated.** The definition carries the whole of H2(a); it sits in Organic Law and has not been drafted or tested.
* **Peripheral voice on shared matters is the normal sortition deficit,** made worse by distance: travel to the centre council is assumed at +25% time and not otherwise modelled.
* Every hour budget, turnout and dispute rate is an assumption; the census figures are as supplied, not re-verified.
