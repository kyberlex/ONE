## **Draft A. Art. 1.4.3** *(batch item 1; SIM-QA-35)*

**Current:**
> 3. To preserve evolutionary adaptability and prevent epistemic stagnation, an inviolable margin of seven percent (7%) of all common fabrication facilities, computational bandwidth, and research laboratories shall remain permanently reserved as a Heretic’s Commons for speculative, non-consensus research without peer gatekeeping. Where application requests exceed available capacity, access is allocated strictly via transparent rotational time sortition among applicants without ideological screening. This reserve carries an inviolable biosecurity boundary: research into synthetic self-replicating lethal pathogens, mass neurological agents, or radiological weapons is categorically barred from the commons. To enforce this boundary without human ideological surveillance or algorithmic thought-policing, all biological synthesis hardware across the commons must incorporate publicly auditable, tamper-evident interlocks that prevent the transcription of sequences listed in a public hazard registry, as specified in the Organic Law Annex. Disabling or bypassing these interlocks constitutes a structural crime against biospheric continuity.

**Draft:**
> 3. No less than seven percent (7%) of all common fabrication facilities, computational bandwidth, and research laboratories remains reserved as a Heretic’s Commons for speculative, non-consensus research without peer gatekeeping. The reserve is never drawn from capacity that Tier 1 requires. Where requests exceed capacity, access is allocated by lot, without ideological screening. Research that produces agents or devices able to cause mass death or irreversible harm to the biosphere is barred from the commons. Organic Law lists the classes of such research and may add one only on certification by rival expert panels (Article 4.4). The bar is enforced by technical means built into the hardware and open to public audit, never by surveillance of persons, human or algorithmic. Disabling or bypassing those means is a structural crime against biospheric continuity.

**How each decision is carried:** 1a, "No less than". 1b, second sentence. 1c, "by lot, without ideological screening". 1d, the bar by function; classes in Organic Law; certification. 1e, the duty kept, the mechanism gone. 1f, last sentence kept.

**Choice made in drafting, for the maintainer.** Decision 1b can be worded two ways. *As drafted:* seven percent of all capacity, and the reserve gives way only where Tier 1 needs that capacity. *The other way:* seven percent of what remains after Tier 1. The second makes the reserve smaller at all times; the first keeps its size and touches it only in a shortage. The first is drafted because it weakens less.

**Red team of the wording.**
* *"able to cause mass death".* A first version said "research able to cause". Almost all microbiology and chemistry is able to. As drafted the bar falls on research "that produces agents or devices" with that capacity. `NARROWED`: "produces" still needs reading in a case.
* *Classes added at 60%.* The functional sentence is the limit, the certification is the gate, and Art. 4.6 reviews. `NARROWED`.
* *"technical means built into the hardware".* Wider than today's "biological synthesis hardware": it reaches any hardware used for barred research. Intended; recorded.
* *The three classes disappear from Chapter I.* They must be the first entries of the Organic Law list on the day of the merge, or the bar has no content. A condition of the merge.

**Words removed or moved** *(instruction 8)*:
| Words | Kind | Where they go |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "To preserve evolutionary adaptability and prevent epistemic stagnation" | a reason, no rule | removed; recorded here |
| "inviolable margin", "permanently", "strictly", "categorically", "inviolable biosecurity boundary" | intensifiers; Art. 1.7 already makes the chapter permanent | removed |
| "transparent rotational time sortition among applicants" | procedure | Organic Law; "by lot" stays |
| "synthetic self-replicating lethal pathogens, mass neurological agents, or radiological weapons" | list | Organic Law, as the first three classes |
| "publicly auditable, tamper-evident interlocks that prevent the transcription of sequences listed in a public hazard registry" | mechanism | Organic Law (OL-1); "open to public audit" stays |
| "ideological surveillance", "thought-policing" | named wrongs | carried as "never by surveillance of persons, human or algorithmic" |

## **Draft B. Art. 1.2.2** *(batch item 5; SIM-QA-41)*

**Current:**
> 2. Coercive genetic alteration, involuntary biometric tracking, neurological monitoring, pharmacological manipulation, and physical subjugation are permanently prohibited as structural violations of personal integrity.

**Draft:**
> 2. Genetic alteration, biometric tracking, neurological monitoring, and pharmacological manipulation without the person's free consent, and physical subjugation, are permanently prohibited as structural violations of personal integrity.

**How the decision is carried:** one condition, "without the person's free consent", now governs the four acts. "Coercive" and "involuntary" are replaced by it: consent obtained by coercion is not free.

**Red team of the wording.**
* *A person who cannot consent.* A child: Draft F says that those who care for a child consent for the child. **An adult who is unconscious or has lost capacity is not covered.** On these words a drug given to such a person is given "without the person's free consent". The canon names nobody who decides for them (SIM-QA-36 D-36.4, the named person; SIM-QA-39 D-39.8, permanent incapacity). `RED_WINS` **until those are decided**: this sentence cannot be final before them.
* *"permanently prohibited" kept.* The words carry the rule here (instruction 11, rule 2).

## **Draft C. Art. 1.2, new point 5** *(batch item 2; SIM-QA-37)*

**Current:** none. The nearest text is Art. 1.2.2 above ("physical subjugation") and, outside Chapter I, Art. 8.5.

**Draft** *(numbered 5 because SIM-QA-22 D-4 takes point 4)*:
> 5. A person may be held against their will only: (a) to stop violence against a person, in progress or immediately about to begin; (b) to stop an act in progress that directly endangers the life of others; (c) after such violence, until a jury drawn by lot validates the holding, within seventy-two (72) hours at most, or the person is released; (d) in custody for predatory violence resisting stabilization, ordered and periodically reviewed by a jury drawn by lot; (e) to carry out the ruling of a tribunal drawn by lot concerning violence. Law may narrow these cases and never widen them. Any other holding of a person is physical subjugation.

**Place left open:** *(f) the protective hold* (batch item 4), and the sentence on the exit of Art. 1.2.3. Not worded: the decision requires a specialist reading first.

**How the decision is carried:** cases i to vi of the batch. Case vii (preparation of mass harm, D-33.4) is not in the list because it is not decided; if accepted it must be added before the lock.

**Choice made in drafting, for the maintainer.** The batch said "custody under Art. 8.5". A reference to Art. 8.5 would let a 75% majority widen custody by amending Chapter VIII. The draft states the substance in Chapter I instead: the ground ("predatory violence resisting stabilization", the canon's words in Art. 8.5.2) and the two guarantees (a drawn jury orders and reviews; 72 hours at most before validation). The number 72 is thereby frozen as a ceiling; law may shorten it.

**Red team of the wording.**
* *(a) "about to begin".* Stretchable. "Immediately" is added. `NARROWED`.
* *(b) "endangers the life of others".* Could be turned against a strike in an essential service. A strike is the refusal of an act, and the draft says "an act in progress" and "directly". `NARROWED`; the reading matters.
* *Care of a small child.* Carrying a child or holding one back from a road is holding a person. Draft F says it is not holding under this point. **Care of an adult without capacity** (a person with dementia kept from walking out at night) has no such sentence and is open (D-39.8). `RED_WINS` until decided.
* *"Law may narrow … never widen".* Restates the ratchet inside the right, so that Chapter VIII cannot add a case.
* *Who holds.* Not said here; it is SIM-QA-37 (D-37.2), in Chapter VIII.

## **Draft D. Art. 1.3, new point 5** *(batch item 6; SIM-QA-38, SIM-QA-30)*

**Current, the two provisions that collide:**
> 1. Every inhabitant holds an inalienable birthright to foundational life support, fully decoupled from wage labor, market exchange, debt bondage, or ideological compliance.

> 1. Every user of the planetary commons incurs an inescapable duty of biophysical stewardship. Human metabolisms must operate within the circular regenerative boundaries and thermodynamic carrying capacity of local watersheds and the global biosphere.

**Draft** *(numbered 5 because SIM-QA-22 D-5 adds point 4)*:
> 5. Where a watershed cannot meet this baseline within its carrying capacity (Article 1.6.1), the order is: first, transfers from other watersheds; second, a bounded and temporary draw on resources that recover, within a margin certified by a panel drawn from outside the watershed; third, a shortage certified by such a panel and shared by all present in proportion to need. Non-renewable resources are never drawn on, and no irreversible harm is done (Article 1.6.2).

**Red team of the wording.**
* *"a margin certified".* The margin is an estimate; the sentence makes a panel answer for it and does not make it true. Residual of SIM-QA-38, unchanged.
* *"temporary", "bounded".* No number: the bound is for Organic Law. Instruction 10 asks that the Constitution keep a bound when a number goes down; here the bound is in words ("resources that recover", "no irreversible harm") and not in a figure. For the maintainer whether that is enough.
* *"shared by all present".* The words of SIM-QA-30 D-30.7: residents and arrivals alike.
* *Effect on Art. 1.6.1.* It is no longer without exception. Disclosure: **weakened**, as decided (item 6).

## **Draft F. The sentence on children** *(SIM-QA-25, point 1; **direction not yet decided**: D-25.1, D-25.2)*

**Current:** none. Chapter I speaks of "every human being" and "every person" and has no text on children.

**Draft** *(proposed as Art. 1.7, new point 3, since it concerns every right of the chapter)*:
> 3. A child holds every right of this Chapter. Those who care for a child exercise these rights, consent included, for the child's benefit and with the child's voice; the child exercises them increasingly as capacity grows. No person and no body owns a child. Caring for and protecting a child is not holding under Article 1.2.5.

**Why it is here before its decision.** Draft B (consent) and Draft C (holding) are unsafe without the second and fourth sentences. If D-25.1 is decided otherwise, those two sentences are still needed in some form.

**Red team of the wording.**
* *"for the child's benefit".* Who judges is the outside panel of SIM-QA-25 point 2, in another chapter.
* *"consent included".* Reaches irreversible, non-therapeutic changes to a child's body. That is D-25.4, open. As drafted, carers could consent to them; a decision for option (a) there needs a further clause here. `RED_WINS` until D-25.4 is decided.
* *One sentence was decided (item 13b); this is four short ones.* The fourth exists only because of Draft C. For the maintainer.

Chapter I with the accepted dockets stood at 1,181 (as recorded in the dockets; not recounted here). With these drafts: **1,436**, which is **236 over** the ceiling of 1,200. The protective hold (not worded) and any sentence from SIM-QA-24, 27 and 28 would come on top. Without Draft F: 1,379.

**Two things this count shows.**
1. **An earlier estimate was wrong.** SIM-QA-35 (section 4.3) and the consolidation said that moving the technical content of Art. 1.4.3 to Organic Law would free "an estimated 60 to 100 words". Drafted, it frees **9**. The decisions on item 1 also added two sentences (the shortage rule of 1b and the certification of 1d), and the reserve, the lot, the bar and the audit all stayed. The estimate was made before any drafting and was marked as such; it should not be relied on anywhere.
2. **The decisions of the batch do not fit the ceiling.** They add about 250 words to a chapter that had 19 left. No point can be cut to meet a ceiling (instruction 10a, point 6), and a ceiling changes only by a separate maintainer decision with a recount of every chapter (point 4). Tightening the wording could save perhaps twenty to thirty words across the drafts; it cannot save 236.

**Decided 2026-11-20:** raise, after a recount; the recount is in [`word_recount_2026-11-20.md`](word_recount_2026-11-20.md), and the figure of 1,200 is suspended until the chapter is fully drafted. The options as first put:

**Options for the maintainer on the ceiling of Chapter I**: **(a)** raise it, after the recount of every chapter that the consolidation already asks for; the chapter that holds the rights no majority can touch is the one where a missing sentence can never be added later. **(b)** Keep 1,200 and reopen the batch: decide which of Drafts C, D, E and F leave Chapter I for a chapter amendable at 75%, knowing why each was placed here (C: otherwise the existing custody is void, or a majority can widen it; D: the two provisions that collide are both in Chapter I; F: Drafts B and C need it).
