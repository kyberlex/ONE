## **Item 1. Art. 1.4.3: the reserve, the bar and the interlock** *(SIM-QA-35; D-35.3)*

**Current text (141 words):**
> 3. To preserve evolutionary adaptability and prevent epistemic stagnation, an inviolable margin of seven percent (7%) of all common fabrication facilities, computational bandwidth, and research laboratories shall remain permanently reserved as a Heretic’s Commons for speculative, non-consensus research without peer gatekeeping. Where application requests exceed available capacity, access is allocated strictly via transparent rotational time sortition among applicants without ideological screening. This reserve carries an inviolable biosecurity boundary: research into synthetic self-replicating lethal pathogens, mass neurological agents, or radiological weapons is categorically barred from the commons. To enforce this boundary without human ideological surveillance or algorithmic thought-policing, all biological synthesis hardware across the commons must incorporate publicly auditable, tamper-evident interlocks that prevent the transcription of sequences listed in a public hazard registry, as specified in the Organic Law Annex. Disabling or bypassing these interlocks constitutes a structural crime against biospheric continuity.

**Problem.** After the lock none of this can be corrected: the figure, the procedure, the mechanism, the list of three classes. A new class of hazard could not be added, because widening the bar narrows the reserve, and the reserve is itself a provision of Chapter I.

| # | Element | Options | Red team |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1a** | "seven percent (7%)" | **A** keep as an exact figure. **B** keep as a floor ("no less than"); Organic Law may raise it. | B |
| **1b** | the reserve in a shortage | Say that the reserve is taken from capacity beyond what Tier 1 needs, so that it never competes with survival: **yes** or **no**. | yes |
| **1c** | "transparent rotational time sortition among applicants without ideological screening" | **A** keep. **B** keep "by lot, without ideological screening"; the rest to Organic Law. | B |
| **1d** | the three barred classes | **A** keep the closed list. **B** state the bar by function (research able to cause mass death or irreversible harm to the biosphere); the classes in Organic Law, which may add one only on certification by rival panels. | B |
| **1e** | the interlock mechanism | **A** keep. **B** keep the duty (the bar is enforced by technical means on the hardware, never by surveillance of persons); the mechanism to Organic Law (OL-1 exists). | B |
| **1f** | "Disabling or bypassing … a structural crime" | Keep. The forum is SIM-QA-33. | keep |

*Cost of 1d B:* a 60% majority could add a class and so narrow the reserve. The functional sentence in Chapter I is the limit, and a review jury applies it (Art. 4.6).
*Effect on the ceiling:* an estimated 60 to 100 words freed; not counted, since nothing is drafted.

## **Item 2. Art. 1.2.2: "physical subjugation" against restraint and custody** *(SIM-QA-37 D-37.8; SIM-QA-35)*

**Current text:**
> 2. Coercive genetic alteration, involuntary biometric tracking, neurological monitoring, pharmacological manipulation, and physical subjugation are permanently prohibited as structural violations of personal integrity.

**And Art. 1.7.2:**
> 2. No assembly, referendum, state of exception, or qualified supermajority may suspend, diminish, or alienate the provisions of this Bill of Rights. Any decree, enactment, or agreement contrary to this Chapter is void *ab initio*.

**Problem.** Holding a person is physical subjugation. On the words, the custody the canon already provides (Art. 8.5) is void, and so is any restraint, even to stop an assault.

| Option | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Leave the text. A review jury decides case by case whether a restraint is "subjugation". Custody under Art. 8.5 stays exposed. |
| **B** | Chapter I itself names, as a closed list, the cases in which a person may be held, and says that outside them holding a person is prohibited. The guarantees stay in Chapter VIII. |
| **C** | Replace "physical subjugation" with a narrower term (for example servitude or bondage). Restraint then falls outside Chapter I altogether and is governed only by Chapter VIII, amendable at 75%. |

*Red team:* **B.** Under C a 75% majority could widen the power to hold people; under A the existing custody is void. With B the list is frozen, so it must be right; its content is item 2-list.

**2-list. If B, which cases enter Chapter I** *(each is also D-37.3)*:
| # | Case | Red team |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| i | stopping violence against a person, in progress | yes |
| ii | violence against a person about to begin | yes |
| iii | holding the person until the validation of Art. 8.5.3 (72 hours) | yes |
| iv | custody for predatory violence under Art. 8.5, with its guarantees | yes (it is the existing canon) |
| v | carrying out a ruling of a tribunal that concerns violence (for example relocation under Art. 8.3.2) | yes |
| vi | an act in progress that endangers the life of others without violence (poisoning a water source; obstruction of life support) | yes, limited to danger to life |
| vii | preparation of mass harm (interlock bypass, weapons): custody without violence so far (D-33.4) | no preference; it is the widest step |
| viii | enforced isolation in an epidemic | see item 3 |
| ix | a protective hold | see item 4 |

## **Item 3. Art. 1.2.3: the epidemic** *(SIM-QA-39 D-39.2)*

**Current text:**
> 3. Every person maintains the inviolable right to bodily autonomy, private intimacy, mental sanctuary, and compassionate, uncoerced biological exit. No civic mandate, collective emergency, or administrative decree may violate the physical or psychological substrate of any individual.

**Problem.** The article names "collective emergency" and refuses the exception. Isolation of a contagious person carried out by force contradicts it.

| Option | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| **O-A** | Isolation by order and support, never by force: full Tier 1 and care delivered, shifts counted as done. Chapter I is not touched. A person who refuses is not reached. |
| **O-B** | Isolation may be enforced, with guarantees. Needs a case in item 2-list (viii) and a qualification of "collective emergency" in this article. |

*Red team:* **O-A.** A test cannot be compelled (it is an act on the body), tracing is voluntary and nobody is recorded, so the measure reaches the willing and the visibly ill whether or not force stands behind it. O-B reopens a choice the authors made on purpose and gains little.

## **Item 4. Art. 1.2.3: the protective hold and the "uncoerced biological exit"** *(SIM-QA-39 D-39.3)*

**Current text:** as in item 3.

**Problem.** The direction of SIM-QA-39 allows a short hold of a person who endangers only themselves when lucid decision is in doubt. That is physical subjugation (item 2), it enters "mental sanctuary", and it interrupts an "uncoerced biological exit".

| Option | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | No hold. Chapter I unchanged. A person in an acute crisis is helped only as far as they accept. |
| **B** | A hold, written into Chapter I as a closed case (item 2-list, ix): an act in progress that endangers the person's own life, with lucidity in doubt; a first period in hours and a maximum in days; no medication imposed; never for words, a refusal of treatment, a protest or a way of living. Chapter I also says that the exit of Art. 1.2.3 is exercised through the procedure of Organic Law (SIM-QA-22 D-9), and that an act outside it may be interrupted for that short time. |

*Red team:* **B, narrowly,** with the trigger and the limits in Chapter I itself so that no majority can stretch them. It is the most delicate item of the batch: once written it cannot be narrowed, and under A it can never be added except as an "expansion", which a hold is not. A specialist reading is needed before the wording, whatever is chosen.

Read strictly they are barred even with consent: the neuro-telemetry of Art. 5.5.3 is void, and a person could not agree to their own medication.

| Option | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Leave the sentence; a review jury reads it. |
| **B** | Make every item of the list depend on the absence of the person's consent (one word moved or repeated). |
| **C** | Bar neurological monitoring outright, even with consent, and delete it from Art. 5.5.3. |

*Red team:* **B.** It adds one or two words and removes a reading under which ordinary care is unconstitutional.

## **Item 6. Arts. 1.3 and 1.6.1: when the floor and the ceiling collide** *(SIM-QA-38 D-38.3; SIM-QA-30 D-30.7, D-30.8)*

**Current text, Art. 1.3.1:**
> 1. Every inhabitant holds an inalienable birthright to foundational life support, fully decoupled from wage labor, market exchange, debt bondage, or ideological compliance.

**Art. 1.3.3:**
> 3. Physical survival shall never be leveraged by any assembly, institution, or collective as an instrument of behavioral conditioning, labor extraction, or social punishment.

**Art. 1.6.1:**
> 1. Every user of the planetary commons incurs an inescapable duty of biophysical stewardship. Human metabolisms must operate within the circular regenerative boundaries and thermodynamic carrying capacity of local watersheds and the global biosphere.

**Art. 1.6.2:**
> 2. The deliberate poisoning of ecosystems, the exhaustion of non-renewable aquifers, and the irreversible destruction of biological habitats constitute crimes against planetary continuity.

**Problem.** Where a basin cannot meet the baseline inside its carrying capacity, the two provisions contradict each other and Chapter I gives no order. A rule elsewhere that chose would be void.

| Option | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Leave both. Each case goes to a review jury, which must resolve doubts "in favor of the person" (Art. 4.6.2): the ceiling would give, without limit. |
| **B** | Chapter I states the order: transfers from other basins first; then a bounded, temporary excess on resources that recover, inside a margin certified from outside; then a shortage certified and shared by all present in proportion to need. Never a non-renewable resource, never irreversible harm (Art. 1.6.2 unchanged). |
| **C** | The ceiling never gives: straight from transfers to shared shortage. |

*Red team:* **B.** It joins the direction of SIM-QA-38 to the decision already taken in SIM-QA-30. C is simpler and stricter and is the honest alternative: the "recoverable margin" of B is an estimate, and a mistake there is irreversible.

## **Item 7. Art. 1.3.2: specialised care** *(SIM-QA-34 D-34.3; SIM-QA-22 D-5)*

**Current text (the health line of the list):**
> * Comprehensive primary, preventative, and emergency healthcare;

**Draft already accepted in principle (SIM-QA-22 D-5), not merged:**
> * Comprehensive primary, preventative, emergency, palliative, rehabilitative, and geriatric healthcare, with care and support for disability;
> 4. Care of the ill, the aged, and the disabled is never cut to raise output elsewhere.

**Problem.** Neither version covers specialised treatment (a transplant, dialysis, cancer care). On the words such care is outside the baseline, and the protection of Art. 1.3.3 does not clearly reach it.

| Option | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Leave specialised care outside Chapter I. The rule that scarce treatment is never allocated by status, contribution or origin is written elsewhere (amendable at 75%). |
| **B** | Chapter I says that access to any treatment the commons provides is never conditioned on status, contribution or origin. It promises equal access, not that every treatment exists. |
| **C** | Add specialised care to the list of the baseline. Every node would then owe it. |

*Red team:* **B.** C promises what a small node cannot supply and would be broken on the first day. B freezes a principle, which is what Chapter I is for.

## **Item 9. Art. 1.5.2: the Foundation's exclusive licence** *(SIM-QA-42 D-42.8)*

**Current text:**
> 2. Scientific discoveries, biological genetics, computational algorithms, and mechanical tool designs are universal public goods, permanently exempt from monopolistic patents, intellectual property claims, and proprietary restrictions.

**Statutes Art. 2.2 (extract):**
> The Foundation exercises an exclusive legal monopoly on licensing these works to the commercial legacy market

**Problem.** An exclusive commercial right held by the Foundation is an intellectual-property claim over what Chapter I exempts from such claims. The works are also published under licences that already allow commercial use by anyone.

| Option | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A** | Chapter I unchanged. The Statutes drop the exclusivity and the "non-commercial" wording; the Foundation may still offer other licence terms for works whose authors assigned that right. |
| **B** | Chapter I unchanged. The Statutes keep the exclusivity and call it openly a transitional derogation toward the outside market. A review jury could void it. |
| **C** | Qualify Art. 1.5.2 for the transition. |

*Red team:* **A.** C writes a commercial exception into the Bill of Rights for good.

**Nothing moves.** One gap was found. Art. 1.8.6 leaves "the longest permitted duration" to Organic Law and states no ceiling for it in the Constitution, and it does not name the body that adopts a measure. Both are the open points R-3 and R-4 of SIM-QA-23. Instruction 10 requires that when a number goes to Organic Law the Constitution keeps its bound. After the lock neither can be added. **R-3 and R-4 must be decided before the lock.**

One overlap to handle in drafting: item 7 (access to treatment never conditioned on "status, contribution or origin") stands beside Art. 1.8.2, where "origin" is already a ground and "contribution" is not.
